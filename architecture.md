# Architecture

## Arena

The game is a server-authoritative multiplayer arena (see `direction.md` and `PRD.md`). Release 1 gameplay has been retired; only compatibility HTTP endpoints and historical data remain.

### Available now: combat, downing, revival and authoritative lives

`/` opens the shared StartScreen and joins a 2400 by 2400 world, packed up to 20 players per room. `/arena` and `/arena/` temporarily redirect to `/`, retaining query parameters. First entry remains an editable draft until `welcome` stores the identity. Permanent names/teams, duplicate-UUID refusal, case-insensitive legacy UUID lookup and live name moderation remain enforced. The one-second moderation scan evicts hidden names with `error` ("That name isn't allowed.") and socket closure; future joins receive the same message.

Players move with WASD/arrows and aim with a world-space mouse cursor; left mouse or Space holds fire. Touch owns independent floating move and aim/fire sticks, with a 6 px dead zone and 60 px radius. Ownership stays with each pointer across screen crossings. Blur, capture loss, rotation, disconnect and life-state transitions clear controls. A fresh tap, Enter, Space or movement key respawns; held inputs at death cannot restart a life. Shared DOM-free view geometry bounds the longest world-view dimension to 900 units, scaling world drawing and mouse coordinates while keeping HUD and sticks in CSS pixels. Bounded camera padding outside the world keeps the local name, barrel and heat feedback clear of the HUD and screen edges at every boundary; movement remains inside the world. The 450-unit spawn inset keeps newly joined robots centred in every supported view.

The pure seeded simulation advances one 50 ms step. `constants.ts` owns tuning: shots travel at 900 units/s, six per second, for 900 ms; barrel offset 25, shot radius 3. Fractional shot cadence and partial-step travel are preserved. Releasing fire keeps an unexpired cooldown and discards overdue fire, so rapid clicking cannot increase the rate or bank a burst. Swept relative-circle collisions pick the earliest eligible bug or glob, with entity-id ties and stable shot order. Players never block shots. An expiry or world exit resets the hit streak once. Each bug impact increases the multiplier by 0.1, capped at 3, and removes one bug HP. Only a lethal hit awards the kind's base points (crawler 10, dasher 25, spitter 30) and a kill to its shooter; earned points survive misses. A surviving hit still credits accuracy and emits a spark with zero points.

Each shot adds one heat. Capacity 18 locks firing for 1500 ms. Heat cools at six per second while released or locked, including fractional lockout steps, and never cools between shots in a held stream. Crawlers live in `bugs/crawler.ts`, imported through the registry: speed 140, radius 12, one hit point, nearest living target with player-id ties. Contact costs one of three hits with one second of immunity, leaves the bug alive and never scores. The third hit downs the player for a full 10 seconds. Life state is explicit (`active` / `downed` / `out`): downed players cannot move, shoot, attract bugs or generate spawn credits. Their shots are cancelled without a miss and their released gun cools. Any active player, regardless of team, within 90 units contributes 50 ms of revive progress per tick; 2 seconds of cumulative contact restores one hit and 1.5 seconds of immunity that firing cannot cancel. Multiple helpers do not accelerate progress. Gaps retain progress. The lexicographically first eligible player id on the completing tick receives 50 points, without an accuracy multiplier or kill event. The revived player keeps the same run, score and streak. Normal movement/combat/damage resolves before contact; newly downed players receive no retroactive contact, and newly revived players cannot chain rescues in that tick. Exact completion at the deadline wins; otherwise the life ends once at timeout.

Wave 1 begins on the room's first step and advances every 30 seconds. Waves are an event/unlock clock and do not add spawn volume. Dashers unlock at wave 3 with a 1/6 share, rising linearly to 1/4 at wave 8. Spitters unlock at wave 4 with a constant 1/8 share, limited to 12 living spitters; their unavailable share transfers to crawlers. Other kinds and bosses remain future items. A seeded weighted mix selects a registered kind only after placement succeeds; the sole crawler mix consumes no additional randomness. Each living player's own life generates spawn credits by integrating a piecewise-linear bugs/second curve: 0.1 at spawn, 4.25 at 60 seconds, 5 at 120 seconds, 6 at 240 seconds, then clamped. Downing, termination, removal and respawn clear that life's credits; revival resumes pressure at the original life age without spawn debt. Requests sample 32 seeded points uniformly by area in a 600–900-unit annulus around that player and choose the valid point farthest from the world centre. This keeps incoming groups toward the arena's outer regions, leaving interior openings for drop-ins. If none is valid, 64 angles at each of three radii provide the same bounded outer-region preference. Candidates must be in bounds, at least 500 units from every living player, and outside every living player's conservative 900-by-900 camera rectangle plus 64 units of padding. Bugs still target the nearest living player, so pressure is shared. Live bugs cap at 150; blocked whole requests are discarded without catchup debt. Stable room slots allocate requests starting after the last successful slot to avoid starvation at capacity.

Initial entry and respawn choose a nonoverlapping point with the greatest nearest-bug clearance from 64 seeded candidates and a 16-by-16 grid, all at least 450 units from every edge. With no bugs, central candidates are favoured. If no candidate has 300 units of clearance, every bug within 300 of the best point is pushed straight outward to 300; coincident centres use deterministic bug-id directions. Only positions change, with no kills or score. New lives receive two seconds of spawn protection, shown as a pale rotating shimmer. Emitting a shot ends that protection before contact resolution; blocked firing does not. Spawn protection is separate from the one-second immunity after damage, which firing never clears. Downed and out connections retain room slots and do not move, shoot or attract bugs. Only out players may respawn.

Protocol v1 adds `respawn` and `life_over`. Inputs carry increasing `seq`, `life` and boolean `touch`; stale-life inputs cannot affect a replacement life. Input and respawn attempts share the rolling 30/s budget and 1 KiB UTF-8 limit. Held inputs, including fire, expire after 250 ms. Full snapshots contain wave, player combat/life state (including status, downed deadline, cumulative revive progress and active contact), bugs, player shots, acid globs and step hit/kill/glob-pop events. Bug events carry kind; glob-pop carries an independent fixed +5. The wire references shot owners by their index in the full player array and expands them to ids before validation/consumption, saving repeated UUIDs. It rounds numbers to three decimals; simulation state stays precise. Bounds are 20 players, 150 bugs, 140 shots, 12 globs and 140 events, within the independently tested 64 KiB server cap. Entity references and numeric bounds are validated on receipt. Snapshots serialize once per room tick, skip backed-up sockets and evict five-second stalls. Terminal results bypass cosmetic snapshot skipping and remain in dead-player snapshots.

`life_over` carries life identity, score, kills, precise simulation seconds, touch attribution and `pending`/`saved`/`failed` save status. Dead snapshots retain that frozen result. The client predicts active movement/facing only, resets prediction and controls across life/status changes and interpolates remote players, bugs and shots by identity. Combat uses one monotonic 100 ms delayed view for entity birth, position, removal and hit/kill effects. Historical membership retains even one-snapshot projectiles until their removal tick; the final interval advances at their last observed velocity. Hit sparks begin at the impact tick; kill bursts begin at the removal tick using the same animation-frame timestamp, and event ids prevent replay. Authoritative health, scores and life-over UI remain immediate. It draws cached robot glows/names, procedural beetles and shot streaks, a bounded kill-burst pool, heat/immunity flashes, wave/score/multiplier/hits HUD and a life-over overlay. Old-life save responses cannot reopen the overlay after respawn. Downed robots lie dim with separate countdown/progress rings and a pulsing Help marker; off-screen teammates receive a CSS-pixel edge arrow. The local downed message and countdown retain the camera and keep terminal UI hidden.

The life-over screen immediately shows the authoritative score and save status with an always-available Play again button. Shared results components present server best, personal standing, points to the next rank (including inside the top 10) and team totals. `arena/results.ts` starts independent reads after a saved or failed acknowledgement and refreshes them every two seconds while visible, using the API's five-second timeout. Each read has its own failure state and retries; failed saves retain their warning while showing previously persisted standings. Overlay generations and per-field request sequences fence late responses across saves, respawns, disconnects and reused life numbers. Fresh matched pointer gestures under 10 px replay; scrolling, cancellation and held keys cannot. The work runs outside the frame loop and server tick. [Results validation](docs/arena-results-validation.md) records mixed-mode scoring, Chrome layouts, stats and live-hide evidence. Further bug kinds and pickups remain later slices.

`src/server/server.ts` owns server admission, sockets, timers and SQLite. `main.ts` supplies configuration/signals. Each spawn creates a server UUID run with `mode='arena'`; existing/HTTP runs default to `release1`. Start/finish and linked events commit atomically and retry idempotently. The stored legacy player-id casing is preserved. Finish writes server score, kills as `squashes`, floored simulation seconds, accumulated touch, team snapshot, empty upgrades and `held=0`. HTTP cannot finish arena runs; release 1 plausibility/review rules stay intact. An ordered between-tick lifecycle writer retains start metadata to recover failed starts, logs failures with run ids, updates save status and invalidates score caches after commits. Disconnect, moderation, stall eviction and shutdown finalize all unfinished lives, including downed ones, once. On SIGTERM/SIGINT, admission and simulation stop synchronously. Active lives finish with end_reason=restart (ordinary deaths use death; departures use disconnect; existing/release 1 rows keep null). Arena writes use zero SQLite lock timeout and a yielding shutdown drain retries lock contention for at most four seconds. Final saved/failed life results precede server_restart and close code 1012; sockets get up to 500 ms to close, with a 4.8-second process watchdog and nonzero exit for incomplete saves. Repeated signals share one drain. Reconnection starts a new life using the same identity and touch attribution, after 0.5, 1, 2, 4, then 5 seconds between attempts. The play view stays visible with truthful restart-save feedback. Vite resolves one build id from a nonempty build-time Railway commit, git HEAD, or UTC timestamp, embeds it in the browser and emits dist/build.json. Production reads that manifest once, ignoring runtime build overrides; without it the server uses development. A changed build triggers a guarded reload, with sessionStorage/history retaining identity and a one-use automatic arena resume marker. Failed reload attempts stay guarded across stale bundles; a welcome matching the requested page build retires those attempts so later rollbacks reload correctly. Pending restart results signal a connection-wide shutdown regardless of whether prediction has received their life number (including immediately after respawn). They freeze prediction and suspend the snapshot timeout until the final acknowledgement/close; ordinary stale death results remain life-filtered, and only committed results claim a saved score. If guard storage fails, reload is suppressed and logged.

`GET /api/arena/stats` reports buildId, populations, rolling nearest-rank tick p50/p95 and drop counters, with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Tick cost includes simulation, serialization and socket enqueueing; scheduler lateness is separate. Closed-room timing expires after a minute; delayed timers advance one step without catchup.

`bun run bots -- --url <owned-origin> --count 18 --seconds 60 --policy aimer` runs real WebSocket clients, validates snapshots, respawns by life identity and reports joins/rates/errors/tick/lateness. Policy defaults to aimer; mover is also available. Both use the same independently seeded policies as `bun run skill`: 20 seeds, eight bots, 180 seconds per policy, pooled life medians including horizon cutoffs. Aimer leads constant-velocity targets, keeps distance, pauses at heat 12 until heat 3, and reads dasher wind-up corridors, retaining a perpendicular escape side through each dash (earliest tell, then id; choose world-edge clearance) and blending the dodge with crowd avoidance only while the corridor threatens; mover wanders/dodges and holds fire along its movement. The regression requires positive medians and aimer at least twice mover. The skill command also measures one newcomer's first life per seed against eight continuously respawning aimers: late joins at 480 seconds must reach 80% of the fresh-join median, and neutral-input medians at every 15-second arena age from 0 through 900 seconds plus solo controls at 0/480 must reach 10 seconds. Censored lives remain lower bounds, with paired windows extended from 600 to 1200/1800 seconds only if needed; inconclusive results fail. The ordinary check retains the existing skill regression, admission-observation tests and the previously failing idle cohorts at 90/150/180 seconds; the complete fairness matrix runs in the skill command. Press **F** for client FPS/update-and-draw cost. The HUD uses one line: score, multiplier, hits, wave and the authoritative connected population in this arena, including dead connections. [Combat validation](docs/arena-combat-validation.md) records skill, production Chrome, simultaneous touch, full-room and 200-player evidence; [movement evidence](docs/arena-validation.md) retains the previous slice's history.

### Release target and extension seams

- **One process:** the existing Bun server also hosts the arenas. `Bun.serve` upgrades `GET /ws` to a WebSocket (Bun's built-in WebSocket, no new dependency). HTTP routes (Hono), SQLite and the static build stay as they are.
- **Shared simulation, server-run:** `src/shared/arena/` holds the arena simulation as pure TypeScript: world, players, bugs, shots, waves, pickups, scoring. It advances by a fixed step (50 ms, 20 Hz) from per-player inputs and a seeded random generator, never reading the clock, `Math.random` or the DOM, so `bun test` covers it by stepping. The server runs it; the client imports only its types, constants and pure helpers (for prediction and drawing).
- **Rooms:** `src/server/arena/` owns the room manager (arenas of at most 20 players; join the fullest arena with room, else open a new one; empty arenas close), the tick loop, the WebSocket protocol and the bridge to SQLite (a life's score is saved when the life ends, and for every live player on shutdown).
- **Protocol:** small JSON messages defined once in `src/shared/arena/protocol.ts`, versioned, validated on receipt (unknown or oversized messages are dropped and counted). Client to server: `hello` (player id, name, team, touch flag, build id), `input` (sequence number, move vector, aim angle, fire held), `respawn`. Server to client: `welcome` (your id, arena id, build id, world size, constants), `snapshot` at 20 Hz (tick, players, bugs, shots, pickups, events since the last snapshot), `life_over` (life, score, kills, seconds, touch, save status, optional reason), `server_restart` (optional finalized restart result), `error`. Snapshots carry only what clients draw; one file per message type is fine.
- **Client:** `src/web/arena/` connects, sends inputs at the tick rate, interpolates other entities about 100 ms behind the latest snapshot and predicts its own robot's movement, reconciling with the server. The camera follows your robot over a world larger than the screen; a minimap shows teammates and the swarm. Rendering reuses the canvas look (procedural drawing, fixed pools, cached glows).
- **Controls:** keyboard + mouse (move, aim, hold to fire) and twin touch sticks (left half moves, right half aims and fires while held), in `src/web/arena/input.ts`.
- **Redeploy safety:** the server sends its packaged build id in `welcome` and stats; on `SIGTERM`/`SIGINT` it saves unfinished lives and sends finalized restart results before closing sockets with code 1012. Clients reconnect with backoff and resume automatically after a guarded changed-build reload. [Redeploy validation](docs/redeploy-validation.md) records production Chrome, persistence and deadline evidence.
- **Bots:** `scripts/bots.ts` (`bun run bots -- --url <base> --count <n> --seconds <s> [--policy mover|aimer]`) connects real WebSocket clients that play with a scripted policy, and reports joins, message rates, errors and the server's tick p50/p95 (the server exposes tick timing on `GET /api/arena/stats`). The same policies run inside the pure simulation for skill measurements in tests.
- **Seams so items don't collide:** one file per bug kind (`src/shared/arena/bugs/<kind>.ts`), per pickup (`src/shared/arena/pickups/<kind>.ts`) and per message type; a registry imports them. Bug extension steps are below; pickups likewise own their implementation, registration and tests.
- **Tests:** the simulation is tested by stepping; the room manager and protocol are tested in process; at least one integration test starts the real server on an ephemeral port (`port: 0`) and connects two WebSocket clients. Close every server and socket before a test ends.

### Bug seams

`src/shared/arena/bugs/registry.ts` is the authoritative kind catalog; `BugKind` derives from its keys. Every `BugDefinition` owns radius, maximum health, base points, initialization, deterministic behavior and kind-specific wire validation. `createBug` supplies required HP/facing and invokes initialization. `createBug` accepts the current simulation time so new kinds receive their full initial attack delay. Optional `phase`/`phaseUntil` are owned by the kind, with timers in simulation milliseconds. A kind can supply `advancePhase` to advance committed phase/timer/velocity state across missed snapshots without predicting health or target decisions; the same callback owns simulation transitions. Its `committedPhases` lists intervals with locked velocity, allowing rendering to advance their exact path before blending later tracking movement, even when the next snapshot removes the bug. Protocol checks common fields and checked registry membership before delegating phase/timer/velocity validation. Hit/kill events carry kind; glob-pop does not. All use at most one event per consumed player projectile, retaining the collection and 64 KiB bounds. Snapshot changes deploy client/server together through the existing build-id reload flow.

The dasher has radius 16, two HP and 25 base points. It approaches at 140 units/s, locks facing inside 240 units, stops for a 700 ms wind-up, dashes at 490 units/s for 450 ms without steering, then recovers at 35 units/s for 1,000 ms. It can take ordinary projectile damage in every phase. The 240-unit trigger is tuned from the suggested approximately 280: the dash travels 220.5 units and contact radii add 30. A committed attack completes even if its target dies. Nearest living targets break ties by id. A world edge stops the whole motion without sliding or changing locked facing; timers continue. Swept projectile and contact checks share reusable per-bug motion, split into travel then a stationary wall segment. Contact uses the player's actual step displacement; kills resolve first, surviving hits remain eligible for contact. Snapshots publish phase and velocity for the following interval after resolving the completed motion.

The spitter has radius 18, two HP and 30 base points. It corrects toward 400 units from the nearest active player, strafes at 35 units/s and caps combined speed at 140. Each 2,200 ms cycle moves for 1,700 ms, then stops and swells for 500 ms. At emission it aims at the nearest active player's current position, without lead. Targetless cycles are consumed. A server-only optional `attack` hook runs once after completed combat, damage and revives, before the next phase publication; movement and client phase advancement never emit entities. Killing a swelling spitter prevents its attack on that boundary.

`globs.ts` owns independent server projectiles: radius 9, muzzle offset 28, speed 320 units/s, lifetime 2,000 ms. They survive source death and disappear on first active-player contact, shot interception, expiry or world exit. Swept relative collisions use actual player displacement and clip to shot/glob lifetime and world exit. Stable player-shot order picks the earliest bug or eligible glob, breaking entity ties by id. Interception at or before glob/player contact wins; a later interception cannot cancel earlier damage. Remaining glob contacts resolve by time, glob id and player id, consuming even when protection blocks damage. Bodies and globs share immunity/downing rules. A pop awards exactly 5 points without multiplying, increments accuracy streak for subsequent hits, and never increments bug kills. No persistence migration is needed. At most twelve globs can coexist: both first-shot delay and repeat cadence exceed lifetime, so all living globs came from distinct spitters alive two seconds ago, including replacements.

Spitter swelling and glob birth/travel/removal use the delayed combat timeline. The swelling visual keeps collision radius unchanged. Cached green glows, a bright nine-unit glob core, a short velocity trail and green +5 interception bursts are procedural and bounded. The aimer checks closest approach over up to one second (clipped to remaining lifetime), holds a perpendicular escape side selected for wall clearance, blends it with crowd/wall/dasher avoidance, and resumes rescues once clear. Mover behavior is unchanged. [Spitter validation](docs/spitter-validation.md) records issue 78's evidence.

`src/web/arena/bugs/registry.ts` is independently checked for every `BugKind`, so the server never imports canvas code. Each draw factory creates cached resources once; the main loop only culls by its registered extent and dispatches. The dasher's magenta angular shell compresses during wind-up, its cached hot glow brightens, and a faint locked-direction line shows the dash path, clipped to world bounds. Culling includes this warning extent. Animation uses the same delayed combat clock as positions, HP, phase changes and sparks; interpolation preserves stationary wind-up and straight dash intervals across gaps into recovery or removal, including stopping the whole motion at the first world edge. Later tracking movement is blended from that committed endpoint. HUD values remain authoritative and immediate.

To add a kind:

1. Put tuning in `constants.ts` and its `BugDefinition` in `src/shared/arena/bugs/<kind>.ts`, with deterministic initialization/movement and phase-aware validation. Movement-only kinds need no attack hook; kinds that emit authoritative entities provide `attack`, which runs only at the server boundary before phase advancement. Register it in the simulation registry.
2. Add its procedural draw factory and culling extent in `src/web/arena/bugs/<kind>.ts`; register it in the draw registry. Cache canvas resources in the factory.
3. Add its wave-dependent weight in `bugs/spawn-mix.ts`. Placement, personal pressure and the live cap remain shared.
4. Test behavior, damage/value, wire states and drawing. Update the maximum-payload fixture and skill/performance evidence where the new state or work changes those bounds.

Movement-only kinds need no edits to combat, protocol, simulation or the main drawing loop. A new attack entity also needs its collision, transport, delayed rendering and policy paths, as the spitter/glob implementation demonstrates. Do not add a generic phase interpreter until actual behaviors require one. [Dasher validation](docs/dasher-validation.md) records issue 71's evidence.


### Entry and screen overlays

`src/web/entry/StartScreen.tsx` shows the title, name and two team buttons; returning players see their name/team and one Play button. Legacy teamless identities choose a team once. Arena owns draft/error state, touch attribution and welcome-only identity persistence. No socket opens before activation except a one-use deploy resume. Rejected fresh names remain editable; accepted identities stay fixed. `src/web/identity.ts` retains the `bug-swarm-player` key and UUID spelling. `src/web/api.ts` reads scores only. `src/web/drawing/` owns the decorative scene/share styles and drawing primitives, independently of simulation.

`arena/minimap.ts` owns projection and all minimap markers so new markers can be added in one place. It preserves world aspect, outlines the world, shows faint bug dots and both living teams, replaces the local interpolated entry with predicted position and draws its bright ring last. It visits at most 20 players and 150 bugs. HUD formatting/measurement is cached until values or viewport change; only overflowing scores use compact notation. Safe-area padding keeps canvas overlays within the usable screen; touch sticks draw above them.

`arena/hint.ts` consumes `bug-swarm-arena-hint-seen=1` on the first living authoritative snapshot. A noninteractive two-line desktop/touch hint fades after six seconds or the first confirmed shot (`spawnProtectedUntil === 0`); natural protection expiry does not dismiss it. Touch can update the visible copy. Death, disconnect and unmount dismiss it; reconnect and respawn never rearm it. Storage failures log once and retain page-lifetime suppression. React updates only on hint transitions. Validation: [root cutover](docs/cutover-validation.md).

## Stack
- Bun (runtime, package manager, test runner), TypeScript strict, Vite for the browser build, Canvas 2D for the game, Hono on Bun for a small API, SQLite through `bun:sqlite` for runs and scores.
- Why: the bootcamp's supported stack, with the same commands on Windows, macOS and Linux. A game loop needs no UI framework; the server runs combat, persistence and the shared boards.
- React is a thin shell for entry, first-life hints and results around the canvas. No React in the game loop.

## How it runs
- Install: `bun install`
- Develop: `bun run dev` (Vite dev server for the game, with the API server beside it)
- Start (production mode): `bun run build` then `bun run start`: one Bun process serves the API and built UI from `dist/` on `0.0.0.0` and `PORT` (default 3000). `bun run dev` keeps the API on `127.0.0.1`. `/board` (also `/board/`) is the stream display in both modes.
- Container: `Dockerfile` builds the game and runs `bun src/server/main.ts`; `DATA_DIR` must be a persistent volume.
- Health: `GET /health` answers `200 ok` once the app is ready.
- Sharing: `/favicon.svg` is a procedural robot; `/favicon.ico` embeds its 32 px PNG, `/apple-touch-icon.png` is 180 px, and `/og-image.png` is a 1200 x 630 arena composition. `src/server/share-assets.ts` generates these once at startup with the server-only `@resvg/resvg-js` rasterizer (no fonts or external resources), then serves cached bytes with `public, max-age=3600`. No generated art files are committed or loaded by gameplay. Vite forwards these exact routes to the API's `PORT`, including before the first build.
- Initial HTML includes the description, dark theme and Open Graph/Twitter cards. `PUBLIC_ORIGIN` takes precedence over `https://` plus Railway's `RAILWAY_PUBLIC_DOMAIN`, then the incoming request URL's origin in production. Configured origins are validated before opening SQLite; forwarded host/proto headers are ignored. Set `PUBLIC_ORIGIN=https://your-domain.example` for custom domains, or `http://your-lan-host:5173` for remote/LAN development. Only HTTP(S) origins are accepted, with an optional trailing slash. Local development needs no environment file: Vite uses its actual frontend URL/port. Built HTML keeps placeholders until the production server substitutes the origin, including `/index.html`, both board routes and SPA fallback; all cards describe the root game URL.
- Test everything: `bun run check` (`tsc --noEmit`, then `bun test`). CI runs exactly this.
- The bet's command: `bun run stats`. Moderation: `bun run review`, `bun run approve <run id>` and `bun run hide "<name>"`. Capacity: `bun run bots -- --url <owned-origin> --count 200 --seconds 120`. Pre-stream cleanup: `bun run reset -- --yes`.

### Container

Build and run with a persistent data directory:

```sh
docker build -t bug-swarm .
docker volume create bug-swarm-data
docker run --rm --name bug-swarm-smoke -e PORT=8080 -e DATA_DIR=/data -p 8080:8080 -v bug-swarm-data:/data bug-swarm
```

Open `http://localhost:8080/`; `http://localhost:8080/health` answers `200 ok`.
The image builds the web bundle and runs `bun src/server/main.ts` directly so Bun receives platform signals. Railway supplies RAILWAY_GIT_COMMIT_SHA as a build argument.
Without `DATA_DIR`, the default relative `data/` directory resolves to `/app/data` in the image.
Persistent deployments should mount their chosen `DATA_DIR`; the example stores `/data/app.db` in the named volume and reuses it across restarts.


## Data and compatibility

SQLite stores players, runs, linked events and hidden names. A run is one life; arena runs carry `mode='arena'`, authoritative combat scores, team snapshots and no upgrade choices. Existing rows and HTTP runs use `release1`. Schema changes remain additive. Public leaders, personal standings and team totals exclude held and hidden scores; personal bests and bet statistics retain historical held runs. Score caches expire within one second; board and results polling refresh every two seconds.

`POST /api/runs` and `POST /api/runs/:id/finish` remain for compatibility only. No shipped browser or workload tool calls them. Their 4 KiB JSON bodies, UUID ownership, fixed names/teams, elapsed-time bounds, upgrade payload validation, idempotency and review holds remain tested. Legacy scores must equal `10 * squashes + seconds`, seconds may not exceed 300 or whole server elapsed time, and squashes may not exceed `floor(4.5 * seconds)`. Compatibility starts charge rolling ten-minute IP/player budgets (300/40); `LOAD_TEST_START_LIMIT` can override the IP limit on a dedicated server. Arena admission uses the WebSocket protocol instead.

Read endpoints include `/api/leaderboard`, `/api/players/:id/best`, `/api/players/:id/standing`, `/api/teams` and `/api/arena/stats`. Standings return `{ rank, total, next }`, with null rank for unranked players and a strict-score gap to the next rank. Ranking ties use earliest eligible finish, then player id. Warm standing lookups use a shared cached player-id map. The board remains isolated at `/board` and `/board/`.

### Owner commands

Stats, hide, review, approve and reset use `DATA_DIR/app.db` (default `data/app.db`). Stats count accepted finishes only, never abandoned starts or legacy opens:

```sh
bun run stats
bun run stats -- --since 2026-11-28T09:00:00-06:00 --until 2026-11-28T10:15:00-06:00
bun run hide "Test Robot"
bun run review
bun run approve <run-id>
```

Filters may be used independently and require ISO timestamps with a timezone. Finish time is server time; since is inclusive and until is exclusive. The four stdout lines are `players`, `finished_runs`, `median_runs_per_player` (one decimal, over players with a finish), and `touch_share` (rounded whole percent). A missing database prints zeros without creating it. Bad flags fail to stderr with no partial report.

Review lists held finished runs oldest first (then run id), including run id, JSON-escaped name, score, submitted seconds, squashes and UTC ISO server finish time. Empty or missing databases print `No held runs`; review does not create a missing database. Approve accepts exactly one UUID, clears only that finished run's hold in an immediate transaction, and reports already eligible runs as a successful no-op. Bad arguments, missing databases and unknown or unfinished runs fail to stderr with exit 1. Both accept the optional `--` separator and close their database handles. Approval changes no score, event, timestamp or name moderation. Public boards, personal standings and team totals observe external approval within five seconds via existing cache expiry and polling, while reads succeed. Compatibility-only HTTP scores strictly above 3,000 require review; authoritative arena lives are saved without a hold.

Hide trims and lowercases the name, hides all players using it, and is idempotent. It accepts now-blocked names and never deletes history. A foreground game-over board observes it within five seconds through cache expiry and polling. Its persisted name key is unchanged by the separate moderation normalization.

Name moderation retains the trimmed 2–16-character ASCII letter/number/space/hyphen/underscore gate and display spelling. It then folds case, removes spaces, hyphens and underscores, maps 0/o, 1/i, 3/e, 4/a, 5/s, 7/t and 8/b, and collapses repeated letters in both names and vocabulary. Normalized blocked terms of four or more characters match substrings; shorter terms match complete names or complete names followed by digits (checked before leet folding), so `ass` is blocked without rejecting `Jason` or `classic`. Abusive compounds such as `badass` have explicit entries. Exact normalized complete-name exceptions allow ordinary collisions such as `Scunthorpe`, `Hancock`, `Draper`, `Sam Hancock` and `therapist`; extensions and numeric suffixes receive no exemption. Reserved names (`colemedin`, `cole medin`, `dynamous`, `archon`, `admin`, `moderator`) use normalized equality before exceptions and return exactly “That name is reserved”; `ArchonFan` and `AdminHelper` remain eligible. Profanity retains “That name isn't allowed. Pick another.” Vocabulary maintenance instructions and the matching tradeoff live beside the list. Updated vocabulary takes effect when the application is deployed, including read-time exclusion of legacy players from both boards, without changing history, personal bests or bet stats. Manual-hide cache expiry and polling remain as above. The entry screen maps both moderation errors to "Pick a different name." and format errors to "Use 2 to 16 letters or numbers." only after a join attempt; editing clears the message. No moderation runs in the frame loop.

`bun run reset` refuses until passed exactly `--yes` (optionally after `--`). The refusal names the resolved database path. A confirmed reset deletes **all** players, runs (including abandoned ones) and events (including legacy opens) in one immediate transaction. It preserves hidden-name moderation, tables and indexes, and prints deleted counts after commit. A missing database succeeds with zeros without creating files. Stop the server before a pre-stream reset and restart afterwards to clear read caches and in-memory rate-limit buckets; unset the load-test override before restarting. ### Pre-stream proxy verification

Use an isolated Railway deployment with scratch data and the same ingress configuration as the stream deployment. Temporarily log the TCP peer, complete `X-Forwarded-For` chain and selected client address at the request boundary. Send requests from a known network with (1) no supplied header, (2) `X-Forwarded-For: 192.0.2.10`, and (3) `X-Forwarded-For: 192.0.2.10, 192.0.2.11`.
