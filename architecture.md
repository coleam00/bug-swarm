# Architecture

## Arena (release 2): the target shape

Release 2 turns the game into a server-authoritative multiplayer arena (see `direction.md` and `PRD.md`). Everything under "Release 1" below describes the single-player game that still runs at `/` until the arena replaces it; do not extend release 1 mechanics.

### Available now: aim, fire and authoritative lives

`/arena` and `/arena/` run the multiplayer combat slice in shared 2400 by 2400 worlds, packed up to 20 players per room. `/` still serves release 1. First entry remains an editable draft until `welcome` stores the identity. Permanent names/teams, duplicate-UUID refusal, case-insensitive legacy UUID lookup and live name moderation remain enforced.

Players move with WASD/arrows and aim with a world-space mouse cursor; left mouse or Space holds fire. Touch owns independent floating move and aim/fire sticks, with a 6 px dead zone and 60 px radius. Ownership stays with each pointer across screen crossings. Blur, capture loss, rotation, disconnect and death clear controls. A fresh tap or gameplay key respawns; held inputs at death cannot restart a life. Bounded camera padding outside the world keeps the local name, barrel and heat feedback clear of the HUD and screen edges at every boundary; movement remains inside the world.

The pure seeded simulation advances one 50 ms step. `constants.ts` owns tuning: shots travel at 900 units/s, six per second, for 900 ms; barrel offset 25, shot radius 3. Fractional shot cadence and partial-step travel are preserved. Releasing fire keeps an unexpired cooldown and discards overdue fire, so rapid clicking cannot increase the rate or bank a burst. Swept relative-circle collisions pick the earliest bug, with bug-id ties and stable shot order. Players never block shots. An expiry or world exit resets the hit streak once. Each hit increases the multiplier by 0.1, capped at 3, before awarding the crawler's 10 points; earned points survive misses.

Each shot adds one heat. Capacity 18 locks firing for 1500 ms. Heat cools at six per second while released or locked, including fractional lockout steps, and never cools between shots in a held stream. Crawlers live in `bugs/crawler.ts`, imported through the registry: speed 140, radius 12, one hit point, nearest living target with player-id ties. Contact costs one of three hits with one second of immunity, leaves the bug alive and never scores. The third hit ends the life immediately; teammate revives are a later item.

Wave 1 begins on the room's first step; each 30-second boundary requests `(6 + 3 * wave) * livingPlayers` crawlers. A half-bug per living player per second trickles between waves. Live bugs cap at 150; blocked requests are discarded. Seeded edge/interior placement keeps 500 units from living players, with bounded grid fallback. Initial entry and respawn choose a nonoverlapping, greatest-clearance point from 64 seeded candidates and a 16 by 16 grid, with one second of immunity. Random spawn candidates leave a 70-unit margin for the robot and heat bar. Dead connections retain room slots and do not move, shoot or attract bugs.

Protocol v1 adds `respawn` and `life_over`. Inputs carry increasing `seq`, `life` and boolean `touch`; stale-life inputs cannot affect a replacement life. Input and respawn attempts share the rolling 30/s budget and 1 KiB UTF-8 limit. Held inputs, including fire, expire after 250 ms. Full snapshots contain wave, player combat/life state, bugs, shots and step kill events. The wire rounds numbers to three decimals; simulation state stays precise. Bounds are 20 players, 150 bugs, 140 shots and 140 events, within the independently tested 64 KiB server cap. Entity references and numeric bounds are validated on receipt. Snapshots serialize once per room tick, skip backed-up sockets and evict five-second stalls. Terminal results bypass cosmetic snapshot skipping and remain in dead-player snapshots.

`life_over` carries life identity, score, kills, precise simulation seconds, touch attribution and `pending`/`saved`/`failed` save status. Dead snapshots retain that frozen result. The client predicts living movement/facing only, resets prediction across life changes and interpolates remote players, bugs and shots by identity. Combat uses one monotonic 100 ms delayed view for entity birth, position, removal and kill effects. Historical membership retains even one-snapshot projectiles until their removal tick; the final interval advances at their last observed velocity. Kill bursts begin at that removal tick using the same animation-frame timestamp, and event ids prevent replay. Authoritative health, scores and life-over UI remain immediate. It draws cached robot glows/names, procedural beetles and shot streaks, a bounded kill-burst pool, heat/immunity flashes, wave/score/multiplier/hits HUD and a life-over overlay. Old-life save responses cannot reopen the overlay after respawn. Minimap, other bug kinds, revives and pickups remain later slices.

`src/server/server.ts` owns server admission, sockets, timers and SQLite. `main.ts` supplies configuration/signals. Each spawn creates a server UUID run with `mode='arena'`; existing/HTTP runs default to `release1`. Start/finish and linked events commit atomically and retry idempotently. The stored legacy player-id casing is preserved. Finish writes server score, kills as `squashes`, floored simulation seconds, accumulated touch, team snapshot, empty upgrades and `held=0`. HTTP cannot finish arena runs; release 1 plausibility/review rules stay intact. An ordered between-tick lifecycle writer retains start metadata to recover failed starts, logs failures with run ids, updates save status and invalidates score caches after commits. Disconnect, moderation, stall eviction and shutdown finalize active lives once. On SIGTERM/SIGINT, admission and simulation stop synchronously. Active lives finish with end_reason=restart (ordinary deaths use death; departures use disconnect; existing/release 1 rows keep null). Arena writes use zero SQLite lock timeout and a yielding shutdown drain retries lock contention for at most four seconds. Final saved/failed life results precede server_restart and close code 1012; sockets get up to 500 ms to close, with a 4.8-second process watchdog and nonzero exit for incomplete saves. Repeated signals share one drain. Reconnection starts a new life using the same identity and touch attribution, after 0.5, 1, 2, 4, then 5 seconds between attempts. The play view stays visible with truthful restart-save feedback. Vite resolves one build id from a nonempty build-time Railway commit, git HEAD, or UTC timestamp, embeds it in the browser and emits dist/build.json. Production reads that manifest once, ignoring runtime build overrides; without it the server uses development. A changed build triggers one guarded reload per target build, with sessionStorage/history retaining identity and a one-use automatic arena resume marker. If guard storage fails, reload is suppressed and logged.

`GET /api/arena/stats` reports buildId, populations, rolling nearest-rank tick p50/p95 and drop counters, with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Tick cost includes simulation, serialization and socket enqueueing; scheduler lateness is separate. Closed-room timing expires after a minute; delayed timers advance one step without catchup.

`bun run bots -- --url <owned-origin> --count 18 --seconds 60 --policy aimer` runs real WebSocket clients, validates snapshots, respawns by life identity and reports joins/rates/errors/tick/lateness. Policy defaults to aimer; mover is also available. Both use the same independently seeded policies as `bun run skill`: 20 seeds, eight bots, 180 seconds per policy, pooled life medians including horizon cutoffs. Aimer leads constant-velocity targets, keeps distance and pauses at heat 12 until heat 3; mover wanders/dodges and holds fire along its movement. The regression requires positive medians and aimer at least twice mover. Press **F** for client FPS/update-and-draw cost. [Combat validation](docs/arena-combat-validation.md) records skill, production Chrome, simultaneous touch, full-room and 200-player evidence; [movement evidence](docs/arena-validation.md) retains the previous slice's history.

### Release target and extension seams

- **One process:** the existing Bun server also hosts the arenas. `Bun.serve` upgrades `GET /ws` to a WebSocket (Bun's built-in WebSocket, no new dependency). HTTP routes (Hono), SQLite and the static build stay as they are.
- **Shared simulation, server-run:** `src/shared/arena/` holds the arena simulation as pure TypeScript: world, players, bugs, shots, waves, pickups, scoring. It advances by a fixed step (50 ms, 20 Hz) from per-player inputs and a seeded random generator, never reading the clock, `Math.random` or the DOM, so `bun test` covers it by stepping. The server runs it; the client imports only its types, constants and pure helpers (for prediction and drawing).
- **Rooms:** `src/server/arena/` owns the room manager (arenas of at most 20 players; join the fullest arena with room, else open a new one; empty arenas close), the tick loop, the WebSocket protocol and the bridge to SQLite (a life's score is saved when the life ends, and for every live player on shutdown).
- **Protocol:** small JSON messages defined once in `src/shared/arena/protocol.ts`, versioned, validated on receipt (unknown or oversized messages are dropped and counted). Client to server: `hello` (player id, name, team, touch flag, build id), `input` (sequence number, move vector, aim angle, fire held), `respawn`. Server to client: `welcome` (your id, arena id, build id, world size, constants), `snapshot` at 20 Hz (tick, players, bugs, shots, pickups, events since the last snapshot), `life_over` (life, score, kills, seconds, touch, save status, optional reason), `server_restart` (optional finalized restart result), `error`. Snapshots carry only what clients draw; one file per message type is fine.
- **Client:** `src/web/arena/` connects, sends inputs at the tick rate, interpolates other entities about 100 ms behind the latest snapshot and predicts its own robot's movement, reconciling with the server. The camera follows your robot over a world larger than the screen; a minimap shows teammates and the swarm. Rendering reuses the canvas look (procedural drawing, fixed pools, cached glows).
- **Controls:** keyboard + mouse (move, aim, hold to fire) and twin touch sticks (left half moves, right half aims and fires while held), in `src/web/arena/input.ts`.
- **Redeploy safety:** the server sends its build id in `welcome`; on `SIGTERM` it ends every life (saving scores) and closes sockets with a restart code; clients reconnect with backoff and reload the page when the build id changed.
- **Bots:** `scripts/bots.ts` (`bun run bots -- --url <base> --count <n> --seconds <s> [--policy mover|aimer]`) connects real WebSocket clients that play with a scripted policy, and reports joins, message rates, errors and the server's tick p50/p95 (the server exposes tick timing on `GET /api/arena/stats`). The same policies run inside the pure simulation for skill measurements in tests.
- **Seams so items don't collide:** one file per bug kind (`src/shared/arena/bugs/<kind>.ts`), per pickup (`src/shared/arena/pickups/<kind>.ts`) and per message type; a registry imports them. Adding a bug or pickup touches its own file, the registry and its test, nothing else.
- **Tests:** the simulation is tested by stepping; the room manager and protocol are tested in process; at least one integration test starts the real server on an ephemeral port (`port: 0`) and connects two WebSocket clients. Close every server and socket before a test ends.

## Release 1 (single-player, being retired)

## Stack
- Bun (runtime, package manager, test runner), TypeScript strict, Vite for the browser build, Canvas 2D for the game, Hono on Bun for a small API, SQLite through `bun:sqlite` for runs and scores.
- Why: the bootcamp's supported stack, with the same commands on Windows, macOS and Linux. A game loop needs no UI framework; the server exists for the shared leaderboard and the bet.
- The scaffolded React screen stays only as a thin shell for name entry, the first-run steering hint, upgrade choices and the game-over screen around the canvas. No React in the game loop.

## How it runs
- Install: `bun install`
- Develop: `bun run dev` (Vite dev server for the game, with the API server beside it)
- Start (production mode): `bun run build` then `bun run start`: one Bun process serves the API and built UI from `dist/` on `0.0.0.0` and `PORT` (default 3000). `bun run dev` keeps the API on `127.0.0.1`. `/board` (also `/board/`) is the stream display in both modes.
- Container: `Dockerfile` builds the game and runs `bun src/server/main.ts`; `DATA_DIR` must be a persistent volume.
- Health: `GET /health` answers `200 ok` once the app is ready.
- Sharing: `/favicon.svg` is a procedural robot; `/favicon.ico` embeds its 32 px PNG, `/apple-touch-icon.png` is 180 px, and `/og-image.png` is a 1200 x 630 arena composition. `src/server/share-assets.ts` generates these once at startup with the server-only `@resvg/resvg-js` rasterizer (no fonts or external resources), then serves cached bytes with `public, max-age=3600`. No generated art files are committed or loaded by gameplay. Vite forwards these exact routes to the API's `PORT`, including before the first build.
- Initial HTML includes the description, dark theme and Open Graph/Twitter cards. `PUBLIC_ORIGIN` takes precedence over `https://` plus Railway's `RAILWAY_PUBLIC_DOMAIN`, then the incoming request URL's origin in production. Configured origins are validated before opening SQLite; forwarded host/proto headers are ignored. Set `PUBLIC_ORIGIN=https://your-domain.example` for custom domains, or `http://your-lan-host:5173` for remote/LAN development. Only HTTP(S) origins are accepted, with an optional trailing slash. Local development needs no environment file: Vite uses its actual frontend URL/port. Built HTML keeps placeholders until the production server substitutes the origin, including `/index.html`, both board routes and SPA fallback; all cards describe the root game URL.
- Test everything: `bun run check` (`tsc --noEmit`, then `bun test`). CI runs exactly this.
- Pacing: `bun run pacing` runs 20 deterministic idle/evasive cases in portrait and landscape. Idle never picks an upgrade: it ends at the first pending choice, or must die inside 18–40 gameplay seconds. Reports distinguish `CHOICE`, death and diagnostic `CUTOFF`, with separate choice counts. Evasive runs select upgrades by priority; their overall and per-shape medians must stay within 60–180 seconds and every evasive run must die by 240 seconds. `bun run pacing --broad` crosses seeds 0–99 with all five full priorities, both shapes and both modes (2,000 runs). It additionally requires complete groups, an overall evasive median of 75–110 seconds and every priority’s evasive median ≥55 seconds. Both commands retain score validation and the legacy `idle60` / `allDead240` gate names; these accept idle's first-choice endpoint, never an arbitrary survivor. Bounds use raw elapsed time with a 1e-7 tolerance. Scores above 3,000 are informational review candidates. Reports print every mode/shape/priority grouping and evasive deaths under 60 seconds. CI keeps the default targets and checks a ten-seed broad slice. See [docs/pacing-validation.md](docs/pacing-validation.md) for definitions and before/after evidence.
- The bet's command: `bun run stats`. Moderation: `bun run review`, `bun run approve <run id>` and `bun run hide "<name>"`. Capacity: `bun run load -- --url <base url>`. Pre-stream cleanup: `bun run reset -- --yes`.

### Container

Build and run with a persistent data directory:

```sh
docker build -t bug-swarm .
docker volume create bug-swarm-data
docker run --rm --name bug-swarm-smoke -e PORT=8080 -e DATA_DIR=/data -p 8080:8080 -v bug-swarm-data:/data bug-swarm
```

Open `http://localhost:8080/`; `http://localhost:8080/health` answers `200 ok`.
The image builds the web bundle and runs `bun src/server/main.ts` directly so Bun receives platform signals. Railway supplies RAILWAY_GIT_COMMIT_SHA as a build argument.
Without `DATA_DIR`, the default relative `data/` directory resolves to `/app/data` in the image.
Persistent deployments should mount their chosen `DATA_DIR`; the example stores `/data/app.db` in the named volume and reuses it across restarts.

## Shape
- Browser game -> HTTP API -> one SQLite file.
- `src/web/entry/StartScreen.tsx`: reusable title, tagline, name/team entry and returning-player Play screen. Its typed `savedIdentity` / `onStart({ name, team, touch })` interface leaves persistence and game activation with the route. It imports its own scoped CSS and validates only after an attempted join using the unchanged server name rules. `StartScene` draws three beetles and a robot with the shared `game/characters.ts` primitives and one cached glow; its loop pauses when hidden, respects reduced motion, and releases frames/listeners/backing stores on unmount. Layout and Chrome evidence: [docs/start-validation.md](docs/start-validation.md). The future `/arena` client can import this component without depending on release 1 game lifecycle code.
- `src/web/App.tsx`: identity persistence and Join/Play activation, the canvas/HUD, the game-over screen with the team race directly under the score. Play again precedes the upgrade summary and top 10 in reading order. Short landscape results use two columns, while other sizes keep a scrolling stack. The short HUD is one compact line; primary-touch play hides the controls footer. The game shell and native upgrade dialog respect safe-area insets. Layout and replay evidence: [docs/landscape-validation.md](docs/landscape-validation.md).
- `src/web/game/`: the game in plain TypeScript.
  - The simulation (robot, bugs, shots, hits) is pure: it advances by a time step from input and seeded state, with no canvas, DOM or clock access, so `bun test` covers it. The first spawn waits 9.3 seconds, independently of subsequent intervals. Spawn intervals interpolate through (seconds, interval): (0, 0.35), (11.7, 0.35), (20, 4), (30, 4), (60, 0.3), (120, 0.2), (180, 0.18). After 180 seconds the interval is `1/6 + (0.18 - 1/6) * exp(-(time - 180)/60)`, approaching six spawns/second from below. Opening pursuit interpolates from multiplier 0.6 at zero to 1 at 15 seconds, without an overshoot. From 15 seconds onward both kinds' movement speeds multiply by `1 + max(0, time - 45)/45 + (max(0, time - 90)/45)^2`, so pursuit keeps accelerating for existing and new bugs. The first scheduled spawn at or after 30 seconds is fast, followed by every fourth eligible spawn. Bug definitions (speed, radius, color and unlock time) and pacing constants live in `src/web/game/config.ts`. Both bug kinds die in one shot. Four upgrade rules extend this same simulation; see the upgrade lifecycle below.
  - `draw.ts` owns the procedural renderer; `assets.ts` caches the full rectangular world's grid and vignette, glows and one eleven-cell score atlas (`+`, digits 0 through 9) on detached canvases. Numeric totals compose cached cells without per-frame text rasterization or a cache per total; labels retain at least an 18 CSS px font. All backing stores release on replacement/disposal. The foreground robot pass follows bugs, shots, particles, vignette, bomb, drones and labels: a cached 88-unit halo, dark-backed mint identity ring (radius 22) and rounded chassis remain readable even without a shield. Cache keys include canvas size, DPR and run geometry. `styles.ts` holds the beetle colours, dimensions and gait for both base and fast kinds; the fast beetle is smaller, brighter and quicker. Collision radii still come from `config.ts`.
  - `fx/` owns fixed-capacity particles (1024), trail samples (128), floating labels (12), hit flash, shield-block flash and shake. Squashes merge into the nearest label within 40 logical world units of its original anchor and 250 ms of its first emission (inclusive; slot order breaks ties). Merges add ten points without moving the anchor or renewing the 650 ms rising/fading lifetime. A full label pool still accepts merges and drops only unmatched cosmetic labels; scoring and particles remain independent. Fixed numeric totals/anchors reuse storage. Other full pools drop new emissions and reuse expired slots. The read-only scored-kill checkpoints and fixed-step comparisons in `fx/feedback.ts` distinguish scored kills from contact damage without changing simulation rules or its random seed. Visual time freezes during upgrade choices, continues after death and resets with focus/run lifecycle.
  - The loop runs on `requestAnimationFrame` with a fixed time step and draws the state to the canvas, scaled to the screen in portrait or landscape.
  - Input: touch uses a floating joystick whose first contact fixes its origin in canvas-local CSS pixels. A radial 6 px dead zone stops movement; speed increases continuously as `(distance - 6) / 54`, reaching full speed at 60 px. The knob is radially clamped to 60 px; the origin never slides, even at an edge or during captured movement outside the canvas. The first accepted pointer owns control until release; other fingers cannot change or take over that contact. Nonzero WASD/arrow direction takes priority, then an active touch (including its stationary dead zone), then mouse/pen targeting. Touch clears the previous mouse target and suppresses mouse movement until release; a fresh mouse event restores targeting. Directional `Input.speed` defaults to 1 and clamps to 0..1 without changing vector normalization; mouse targets retain their no-overshoot stopping. F shows the rolling FPS, current bug/live-effect counts and update/draw-time readout, refreshed once a second while visible. Live effects count particles, trail samples and floating labels, plus one each for an active bomb ring, hit flash, shield-block flash and shake; shots, drones, charged shield outlines and the joystick are excluded. Each run samples the browser viewport at start: its arena keeps 360,000 logical units squared and follows the viewport aspect ratio, clamped to 9:16 through 16:9. Rotation rescales the active arena and cancels touch until a fresh down; the next run adopts the new shape. Desktop steering survives resize.
  - The joystick's translucent 60 px ring and 12 px knob draw after restoring the world's transform and clip, in CSS coordinates under only the DPR transform. They do not shake or receive the vignette, and naturally clip at canvas edges. Two procedural circles add constant work independent of swarm size; no React frame updates or new caches are involved. Evidence: [docs/touch-validation.md](docs/touch-validation.md).
  - The API client starts a run and submits its score without blocking the loop; failures are logged and the game-over screen says the score wasn't saved. A refused start (HTTP 429) instead says “Too many runs right now. Your next run will save.” Restart remains available after the 800 ms input guard; that copy does not guarantee admission or saving on the next attempt.
- `src/web/game/session.ts`: asynchronous run lifecycle, immutable finish payloads, save/read status and polling. Gameplay starts immediately. Saving waits until monotonic time since the server's start acknowledgement covers the submitted gameplay seconds, a conservative lower bound on server elapsed time that accommodates registration latency without changing scores or adding server slack. After the 800 ms input guard, replay never waits for an earlier run's save. Finish acknowledgement is `{ saved: true, held: boolean }`. A held save displays `Under review` while retaining the result, private best, build and replay. It refreshes reads just like any saved result. While a save is held, the existing two-second game-over poll repeats the immutable finish request: identical retries return the persisted hold without writing another finish or event. Approval changes the status to `Score saved`, then these status checks stop. Failed status reads are logged and retain the durable save; old held replies and replies for a previous run cannot overwrite approval or replay. Best, standing, leaderboard and team totals refresh independently on death and acknowledged save; standing, leaderboard and teams poll every two seconds while subscribed on game over. Late responses and start-refusal reasons stay attached to their original local run, and only a newer applied response supersedes an older response. Refused starts never submit a finish. Restart and detachment stop polling. Requests time out after five seconds; no offline submission queue or rate-limit cooldown.
- `src/web/route.ts`: dynamically loads the stream board for `/board` and `/board/`, or the game elsewhere. The board never imports the game screen, engine, identity storage or game CSS.
- `src/web/page.css` prevents page overscroll on both views. Game CSS prevents UI selection/callouts while retaining name editing, canvas steering and scrolling result/upgrade overlays; buttons use `touch-action: manipulation`. The board retains ordinary vertical scrolling. Chrome emulation evidence and its WebKit limitation: [docs/share-validation.md](docs/share-validation.md).
- `src/web/board/`: read-only React stream page with the server's top 10 and Tabs vs Spaces totals. `model.ts` validates snapshots, preserves returned ordering, maps proportional race bars and owns three-second arrival/overtake highlights. `polling.ts` reads both endpoints immediately and every two seconds, independently; the shared API client's five-second deadline bounds overlapping reads. Only newer applied responses supersede older results. Failures retain that endpoint's last successful snapshot and retry after 2, 4, then 8 seconds (capped), with a small reconnecting note; recovery resets the cadence. Missing data shows waiting placeholders, distinct from successful empty/zero data. Cleanup invalidates pending responses and clears timers. The join link uses the page's own origin, in a prominent 36 px desktop join line. At 1280 px and wider, two ordered columns pair 40 px names with right-aligned 40 px scores on one line, above which team totals remain 64 px. Narrower screens use a scrolling column and allow long names to wrap beside their scores. Evidence and isolation limitations: [docs/board-validation.md](docs/board-validation.md).
- `src/server/app.ts`: `/health`, `POST /api/runs`, `POST /api/runs/:runId/finish`, `GET /api/leaderboard`, `GET /api/teams` (`{ tabs, spaces }` numeric totals), `GET /api/players/:playerId/best`, `GET /api/players/:playerId/standing`, and the legacy `GET /api/events/count`.
- `src/server/db.ts`: the SQLite file, its tables and `EVENT_TYPES` (`run_started`, `run_ended`). It re-exports `TEAMS` from `src/shared/teams.ts`, the browser-safe registry of ids, names and colours (Tabs orange `#ff9f43`, Spaces teal `#2ed3c6`). Schema changes are additive.
- `src/server/rules.ts`: name rules and score plausibility (4.5 squashes per submitted gameplay second, at most 300 seconds, bounded by server elapsed time), and a separate owner moderation threshold (`REVIEW_SCORE = 3000`, strictly greater-than holds), all pure and tested.
- `src/server/blocked-words.ts`: the curated blocked vocabulary, reserved names and complete-name exceptions; `rules.ts` shares validation between browser entry, saved identities, board snapshots and server reads/writes.
- `src/server/main.ts`: production entry; reads `PORT` and `DATA_DIR` (default `data/`).
- `scripts/stats.ts`, `scripts/hide.ts`, `scripts/review.ts`, `scripts/approve.ts`, `scripts/load.ts`, `scripts/reset.ts`: owner commands, capacity harness and confirmed gameplay reset.
- Tests are `*.test.ts` beside the code.

## Data
- **Player:** a random UUID the browser creates on first visit and keeps in local storage, with a validated name and a permanent team. No account. Names are fixed for an id; clearing storage creates a new player. Saved identities whose names fail current moderation require fresh name/team entry under a new UUID. Valid legacy identities keep their UUID/name and choose a team once on next entry; invalid stored teams also require selection. A returning identity with a team displays its name, team and one Play button. Loading entry creates no Session, starts no API run and consumes no steering-hint flag. Joining or pressing Play stores locally and starts immediately, even offline or when storage fails; touch entry reaches the initial run. Authoritative team recovery updates the existing Session without remounting gameplay. Every start sends the saved team so an offline selection can reach the server later.
- **Start:** an immediate transaction validates the fixed name, assigns membership, snapshots the team on the run and records its event. New server players require a team (400 if absent); supplied invalid values including null return 400. Existing teamless players may omit it and still play, or assign it once. Assigned players may omit or repeat their team; name or team changes return 409. Failed starts roll back membership, run and event together.
- **Conflicting browser selections:** a team conflict returns 409 with `code: "team-conflict"` and the stored `team`. Session adopts that authoritative team, updates the browser identity without changing UUID/name, and retries the same local run's start once. This repairs stale entry tabs and already-conflicted saved identities, including on reload. Other start errors are not retried. Simulation starts immediately throughout; offline play and in-memory identity retention on storage failure still apply. The server never switches membership.
- **Run:** `run_started` (run id, player id, server time, nullable team snapshot) and `run_ended` (run id, score, squashes, seconds, upgrades, touch or not). A run with no `run_ended` was abandoned. Additive nullable `players.team` and `runs.team` columns restrict values to the shared registry. Existing rows stay null with no backfill: historical runs and runs started while teamless never count, even if finished after assignment. Finish cannot supply team attribution.
- **Review holds:** additive `runs.held` is a non-null 0/1 flag, defaulting to zero. Finish writes the flag, finish fields and event atomically after plausibility validation. When the column is first added, finished scores above the ceiling are held, preserving every other field and unfinished run. Ordinary reopen never backfills again, so approval survives restart. Identical finish retries return the persisted flag, including after approval; conflicting retries still return 409. Holds retain personal bests and bet stats, and remain visible to the operator even for hidden or now-blocked players. No approval history is stored.
- **Hidden names:** a table the top 10, personal standings and the board filter on; `bun run hide` adds to it.
- **Top 10:** one best eligible (`held = 0`) score per player, excluding hidden names and names that fail current validation before taking ten. Ties go to the earliest eligible finish achieving that best, then player id. The shared ranking snapshot holds the top ten, eligible count and a player-id map of standings. It expires at one second or on backwards clock movement and is invalidated after a committed finish; game-over clients poll every two seconds. Snapshot rebuilds scan the eligible players once; warm standing lookups are O(1), and unknown ids never grow the map. Failed rebuilds publish nothing. Personal bests and bet stats retain held runs and hidden, blocked and reserved players; board reads never delete or rewrite players, runs or events.
- **Personal standing:** `GET /api/players/:playerId/standing` returns `{ rank, total, next }`, independently of personal best and finish acknowledgement. Ranking is ordinal, one-based, by the same best-score ordering as the top ten; finished zero scores and legacy teamless players count, unfinished players do not. Duplicate valid names remain separate players. For rank R > 1, `next` is `{ rank: R - 1, pointsToBeat: previousBest - ownBest + 1 }`, so tied scores need one point; rank one has no target. Valid unknown, unfinished, held-only, hidden and currently invalid-name players receive `rank: null`, `next: null` and the visible population count; malformed ids return 400. Read failures are logged 503s with `Cache-Control: no-store`, separate from saves and private bests. Game over shows the best rank and next target (or top-ten encouragement), loading/unavailable/unranked feedback and a persistent own-row highlight matched by player id. Scores and standing numbers use exact en-US separators; game-over names remain one line with visual ellipsis and their full accessible text. Replay retains its 800 ms guard and never waits for ranking. Layout, load and moderation evidence: [docs/standing-validation.md](docs/standing-validation.md).
- **Team race:** sums every finished, unheld run with an eligible team snapshot, excluding hidden names and names that fail current validation (including duplicate names). SQL groups by player id/name and run-team snapshot; current name rules filter those groups at read time before adding them to team totals. It includes all eligible scores, rather than personal bests or only ten players. Both keys appear as zero for an empty database. Totals have their own one-second cache, also expiring on backwards clock movement and invalidated on finish; all API reads use `Cache-Control: no-store`. Separate-process moderation appears through expiry and foreground polling within five seconds while reads succeed. On game over, team read failures clear the race and show unavailable text without affecting saves or restart. The stream board retains last-good totals during outages. Zero totals show a neutral equal split; positive totals use proportional bars with labels outside the segments. Chrome phone layout and moderation evidence: [docs/team-validation.md](docs/team-validation.md).
- **Persistence:** additive `players`, `runs`, `hidden_names` tables plus linked `events`, retaining legacy `opened` events. Start and finish each transact the run and its event together. Identical finish retries succeed; conflicting retries return 409. WAL and a five-second busy timeout support the owner commands alongside the server.

### Owner commands

Stats, hide, review, approve and reset use `DATA_DIR/app.db` (default `data/app.db`). Stats count accepted finishes only, never abandoned starts or legacy opens:

```sh
bun run stats
bun run stats -- --since 2026-11-28T09:00:00-06:00 --until 2026-11-28T10:15:00-06:00
bun run hide "Test Robot"
bun run review
bun run approve <run-id>
```

Filters may be used independently and require ISO timestamps with a timezone. Finish time is server time; since is inclusive and until is exclusive. The four stdout lines are `players`, `finished_runs`, `median_runs_per_player` (one decimal, over players with a finish), and `touch_share` (rounded whole percent). A missing database prints zeros without creating it. Bad flags fail to stderr with no partial report.

Review lists held finished runs oldest first (then run id), including run id, JSON-escaped name, score, submitted seconds, squashes and UTC ISO server finish time. Empty or missing databases print `No held runs`; review does not create a missing database. Approve accepts exactly one UUID, clears only that finished run's hold in an immediate transaction, and reports already eligible runs as a successful no-op. Bad arguments, missing databases and unknown or unfinished runs fail to stderr with exit 1. Both accept the optional `--` separator and close their database handles. Approval changes no score, event, timestamp or name moderation. Public boards, personal standings and team totals observe external approval within five seconds via existing cache expiry and polling, while reads succeed. See [docs/pacing-validation.md](docs/pacing-validation.md) for threshold calibration and Chrome evidence.

Hide trims and lowercases the name, hides all players using it, and is idempotent. It accepts now-blocked names and never deletes history. A foreground game-over board observes it within five seconds through cache expiry and polling. Its persisted name key is unchanged by the separate moderation normalization.

Name moderation retains the trimmed 2–16-character ASCII letter/number/space/hyphen/underscore gate and display spelling. It then folds case, removes spaces, hyphens and underscores, maps 0/o, 1/i, 3/e, 4/a, 5/s, 7/t and 8/b, and collapses repeated letters in both names and vocabulary. Normalized blocked terms of four or more characters match substrings; shorter terms match complete names or complete names followed by digits (checked before leet folding), so `ass` is blocked without rejecting `Jason` or `classic`. Abusive compounds such as `badass` have explicit entries. Exact normalized complete-name exceptions allow ordinary collisions such as `Scunthorpe`, `Hancock`, `Draper`, `Sam Hancock` and `therapist`; extensions and numeric suffixes receive no exemption. Reserved names (`colemedin`, `cole medin`, `dynamous`, `archon`, `admin`, `moderator`) use normalized equality before exceptions and return exactly “That name is reserved”; `ArchonFan` and `AdminHelper` remain eligible. Profanity retains “That name isn't allowed. Pick another.” Vocabulary maintenance instructions and the matching tradeoff live beside the list. Updated vocabulary takes effect when the application is deployed, including read-time exclusion of legacy players from both boards, without changing history, personal bests or bet stats. Manual-hide cache expiry and polling remain as above. The entry screen maps both moderation errors to "Pick a different name." and format errors to "Use 2 to 16 letters or numbers." only after a join attempt; editing clears the message. No moderation runs in the frame loop.

`bun run reset` refuses until passed exactly `--yes` (optionally after `--`). The refusal names the resolved database path. A confirmed reset deletes **all** players, runs (including abandoned ones) and events (including legacy opens) in one immediate transaction. It preserves hidden-name moderation, tables and indexes, and prints deleted counts after commit. A missing database succeeds with zeros without creating files. Stop the server before a pre-stream reset and restart afterwards to clear read caches and in-memory rate-limit buckets; unset the load-test override before restarting. Reset does not selectively remove `load-` names.

### Capacity validation

`bun run load -- --url http://127.0.0.1:43125 [--players 500] [--seconds 60]` writes one new `load-` player and one run per simulated player. The URL must be an HTTP(S) origin. Players start concurrently, die after 5–15 seconds (capped at one-third of shorter invocations), and finish with conservative elapsed-time scores accepted by the normal plausibility rules. At death and acknowledged save they read personal best, standing, top 10 and team totals. Standing, top 10 and teams then poll every two seconds through the deadline; a separate stream-board observer reads leaderboard and teams immediately and every five seconds. The observer follows the PRD data cadence without downloading `/board` HTML. This compressed HTTP workload leaves time for sustained game-over polling; it does not simulate a full gameplay duration.

Each request measures through the entire response body, has a five-second timeout, refuses redirects and validates its expected status and essential JSON shape. At the deadline no new requests start; in-flight requests drain within their timeouts. SIGINT/SIGTERM stop scheduling and print a failing partial summary. Output includes requested/accepted players, total requests, failures by HTTP status or timeout/transport/invalid-response category, and count/p50/p95/p99 latency by route and overall (nearest rank over raw samples, including failures). Empty percentiles are `n/a`. A pass requires every player to start and finish, successful observations of every read route, no failed requests, zero 5xx and at least 95% of all requests succeeding in strictly under 200 ms. Failures or an incomplete workload exit nonzero.

The defaults are 300 starts per client IP and 40 per player id per rolling ten minutes. For a dedicated benchmark server only, `LOAD_TEST_START_LIMIT=10000` explicitly raises the IP ceiling, also in production mode; the player ceiling remains 40. It accepts only positive safe integers and is validated before opening the database; limits above 300 print a startup warning. Restart with a fresh scratch database between smoke and measured runs (reset alone does not clear the limiter). Reproduction commands and factory measurements: [docs/load-validation.md](docs/load-validation.md). The timed benchmark is manual; `bun run check` runs its short deterministic tests only.

## Boundaries
- No API keys and no outside services. The app starts and `bun run check` passes with no `.env`.
- Public on the internet during the stream: the API accepts only well-formed requests with capped sizes, at most one score per run id, scores that pass the plausibility check, and at most 300 start attempts per client IP and 40 per player id per rolling 10 minutes (`START_LIMIT`, `PLAYER_START_LIMIT` and `START_WINDOW_MS` in `app.ts`).
- JSON bodies are capped at 4 KiB. Scores must equal `10 * squashes + seconds`. Submitted gameplay seconds must not exceed either whole server elapsed seconds or 300 seconds; squashes must not exceed `floor(4.5 * submitted seconds)`. Choice pauses earn no additional squash allowance, and wall-clock age has no cap. Constants live in `rules.ts`; calibration is recorded in [docs/pacing-validation.md](docs/pacing-validation.md#score-plausibility-calibration-issue-34).
- Peer IPs come from Bun. `TRUST_PROXY_HOPS` defaults to `0`: forwarded headers are ignored unless `TRUSTED_PROXIES` lists the exact peer address. That legacy comma-separated trust list walks the header from the right while the current address is trusted; avoid trusting public client addresses. Positive hop counts take precedence over that list and select the n-th address from the right: for `192.0.2.10, 198.51.100.20, 10.0.0.9`, `1` selects `10.0.0.9`, and `2` selects `198.51.100.20`. Prepending forged entries cannot change selection at a fixed trusted depth. The setting accepts only canonical nonnegative decimal safe integers; invalid values fail before SQLite opens. Missing, malformed or too-short headers fall back to the original peer without throwing. Every entry must be a literal IPv4/IPv6 address; empty entries, hostnames, ports, brackets and zone identifiers invalidate the entire chain without shifting positions.
- Positive hops assume **all** traffic reaches the app through that trusted proxy depth, with the proxy appending the actual caller address. Never enable this on a directly exposed listener or an unverified shorter ingress path: a direct caller could supply the selected entry. This is deployment trust, not authentication.
- After bounded body handling, each start checks and charges the IP budget before JSON parsing. A valid UUID then checks and charges its lowercase player-id bucket before name/team validation or database writes. Invalid requests consume IP attempts; valid-ID attempts also consume player attempts; player refusals consume an IP attempt. Refused buckets do not gain timestamps. Both limits return HTTP 429 with `Retry-After` in seconds (rounded up from the oldest live attempt, at least one); IP checks win if both are exhausted. Attempts at or before the ten-minute boundary expire. Refusals create no players, runs or events. Finish and board routes remain usable, including saving already-issued runs. Both budgets are in memory and reset on process restart.
- Nothing the player does depends on a server response; a run plays with the API down.

## Upgrade contract and choices

The first locally started arena shows a nonblocking DOM steering hint: “Drag anywhere to move” for touch entry or a coarse pointer, otherwise “WASD, arrows or mouse”. `steering-hint.ts` consumes a separate `bug-swarm-steering-hint-seen=1` localStorage flag at local start, including for existing identities after deployment, without waiting for registration. Entry and `/board` do not consume it. Storage getter/read/write failures log once and retain page-lifetime suppression. Restart never rearms the hint.

The controller retains its initial run token and four-second monotonic wall-time deadline across effect detachment/reattachment. A one-shot timer and foreground deadline checks expire it; actual robot displacement, death, a choice or token replacement dismiss it sooner. While pending, `mountGame` compares position around the existing fixed-step advance and reports movement once; it removes observation after dismissal. Touch-down, dead-zone or ignored input, paused frames and resize do not dismiss it. React receives only hint transitions. Chrome layout, offline, persistence and frame-cost evidence: [docs/steering-validation.md](docs/steering-validation.md). Both pacing matrices pass with the owner's idle definition and the opening's monotonic 60%-to-normal speed ramp; current and historical reports are linked from the pacing validation document.

`src/shared/upgrades.ts` owns the four ids, names and 20-second interval. Finish requests carry acquired upgrades as `{ id, count }` entries, once each in registry order. Counts record picks, independent of remaining combat resources. The server accepts legacy omitted upgrades as `[]`, validates total picks against survival seconds, normalizes ordering and includes the normalized build in identical-retry comparisons. The existing `runs.upgrades` JSON column and its linked `run_ended` event commit together. No additional event payload or migration is needed. Choice pauses need no server-clock change: elapsed time is an upper bound.

`game/upgrades.ts` maintains counts and three distinct seeded offers. Its independent LCG starts at `seed XOR 0x9e3779b9`, leaving spawn randomness untouched. Choice numbers reject delayed selections. The simulation clamps steps at each 20-second gameplay boundary, resolves that interval, and offers a choice only if still alive. Pending choices freeze all gameplay and cooldowns. Selection applies the upgrade synchronously, increments the next boundary and rejects stale run tokens and choice numbers. There is no run cutoff.

The fixed stepper drops catch-up accumulation at choices and resets its timestamp on resume. Feedback receives the actual shortened active duration. The engine clears steering and pointer capture on either transition, freezes visual time during choices and resets the effects clock on resume. Blur/visibility/rotation preserve the pending choice. Session subscriptions publish only transitions and guard readiness, not combat frames.

Release, cancellation, lost capture, blur, visibility changes, either choice transition, death, restart, run replacement and unmount clear touch ownership and its overlay. Ownership is cleared before releasing capture, making resulting lost-capture events harmless. Delayed touch moves cannot revive input without a new down. Death retains held-key restart protection and advances its existing effects; a fresh touch can restart after the 800 ms guard but stays stationary until dragged.

React displays a native modal dialog with three large button cards, no countdown or dismissal, focus containment and focus restoration to the canvas. Cards fade in for 350 ms and expose aria-disabled during that guard while remaining focusable. Reduced motion omits the fade, retaining the delay. Fresh 1-3 keys and touch cards share Session.choose; touch selections mark input attribution. Finish and game-over use the same deeply frozen count summary, isolated from restarts and late API responses.

Session owns monotonic deadlines: 800 ms from death before restarting and 350 ms from the first publication of each choice before selecting. Eligibility is inclusive at the deadline and checked again by Session on every activation; published readiness is only for presentation. Initial entry remains immediate. A single injected one-shot scheduler publishes readiness while subscribed, reschedules only the remainder if called early, and cancels on replacement, selection, superseding death or final detachment. Deadlines survive detach/reattach; stale callbacks are harmless. Readiness does not use the frame loop, reset board polling or make network requests.

Game-over results render immediately, with space reserved for the replay instruction and button until the guard expires. Canvas, overlay, keyboard and assistive activations all pass through Session.restart. Overlay and card gestures must begin after eligibility; early downs released after the deadline do nothing. Early activation keys remain blocked through release, including native Space keyup clicks; fresh presses work afterward. Rejected inputs never attribute touch, steer, acquire capture, start an API request or queue an action. Choice gameplay remains frozen throughout. Chrome evidence and reproduction: [docs/guard-validation.md](docs/guard-validation.md).

Contact can consume a shield charge or health and grants one second of immunity, but never removes the attacker. Bugs remain alive until a projectile or bomb kills them; moving away ends contact without a free clear. This keeps immunity from providing passive crowd control, and only weapon kills award squashes.

Review laser gives new shots `1 + picks` penetrations and a longer procedural streak. Combat and the first card share `laserFireMultiplier` in `src/shared/upgrades.ts`; later cards describe additional penetration.
