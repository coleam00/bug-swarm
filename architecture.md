# Architecture

## Arena

The game is a server-authoritative multiplayer arena (see `direction.md` and `PRD.md`). Release 1 gameplay has been retired; only compatibility HTTP endpoints and historical data remain.

### Available now: combat, pickups, downing, revival and authoritative lives

`/` opens the shared StartScreen and joins a 2400 by 2400 world, packed up to 20 players per room. `/arena` and `/arena/` temporarily redirect to `/`, retaining query parameters. First entry remains an editable draft until `welcome` stores the identity. Permanent names/teams, duplicate-UUID refusal, case-insensitive legacy UUID lookup and live name moderation remain enforced. The one-second moderation scan evicts hidden names with `error` ("That name isn't allowed.") and socket closure; future joins receive the same message.

Players move with WASD/arrows and aim with a world-space mouse cursor; left mouse or Space holds fire. Touch owns independent floating move and aim/fire sticks, with a 6 px dead zone and 60 px radius. Ownership stays with each pointer across screen crossings. Blur, capture loss, rotation, disconnect and life-state transitions clear controls. A fresh tap, Enter, Space or movement key respawns; held inputs at death cannot restart a life. Shared DOM-free view geometry bounds the longest world-view dimension to 900 units, scaling world drawing and mouse coordinates while keeping HUD and sticks in CSS pixels. Bounded camera padding outside the world keeps the local name, barrel and heat feedback clear of the HUD and screen edges at every boundary; movement remains inside the world. The 450-unit spawn inset keeps newly joined robots centred in every supported view.

The pure seeded simulation advances one 50 ms step. `constants.ts` owns tuning: shots travel at 900 units/s, normally six per second, for 900 ms; barrel offset 25, shot radius 3. Fractional shot cadence and partial-step travel are preserved. Releasing fire keeps an unexpired cooldown and discards overdue fire, so rapid clicking cannot increase the rate or bank a burst. Swept relative-circle collisions pick the earliest eligible bug or glob, with entity-id ties and stable shot order between glob-contact boundaries. Players never block shots. An expiry or world exit resets the hit streak once. Each damaging bug impact increases the multiplier by 0.1, capped at 3, and removes one bug HP. A blocked impact consumes the projectile, resets its owner's streak once and emits a zero-point shell bounce without changing HP or earned points. Only a lethal hit awards the kind's base points (crawler 10, dasher 25, spitter 30, beetle 60) and a kill to its shooter; earned points survive misses. A surviving hit still credits accuracy and emits a spark with zero points.

Each shot adds one heat. Capacity 18 locks firing for 1500 ms. Heat cools at six per second while released or locked, including fractional lockout steps, and never cools between shots in a held stream. Crawlers live in `bugs/crawler.ts`, imported through the registry: speed 140, radius 12, one hit point, nearest living target with player-id ties. Contact costs one of three hits with one second of immunity, leaves the bug alive and never scores. The third hit downs the player for a full 10 seconds. Life state is explicit (`active` / `downed` / `out`): downed players cannot move, shoot, attract bugs or generate spawn credits. Their shots are cancelled without a miss and their released gun cools. Any active player, regardless of team, within 90 units contributes 50 ms of revive progress per tick; 2 seconds of cumulative contact restores one hit and 1.5 seconds of immunity that firing cannot cancel. Multiple helpers do not accelerate progress. Gaps retain progress. The lexicographically first eligible player id on the completing tick receives 50 points, without an accuracy multiplier or kill event. The revived player keeps the same run, score and streak. Normal movement/combat/damage resolves before contact; newly downed players receive no retroactive contact, and newly revived players cannot chain rescues in that tick. Exact completion at the deadline wins; otherwise the life ends once at timeout.

Wave 1 begins on the room's first step and advances every 30 seconds. Waves are an event/unlock clock and do not multiply ordinary spawn volume. Scheduled hive encounters add their own bounded brood. Dashers unlock at wave 3 with a 1/6 share, rising linearly to 1/4 at wave 8. Spitters unlock at wave 4 with a constant 1/8 share, limited to 12 living spitters; their unavailable share transfers to crawlers. Armoured beetles unlock at wave 5 with a constant 1/10 share and a ten-live cap, transferring unavailable beetle weight to crawlers. Hive bosses arrive on every fifth wave; other kinds remain future items. A seeded weighted mix selects a registered kind only after placement succeeds; the sole crawler mix consumes no additional randomness. Each living player's own life generates spawn credits by integrating a piecewise-linear bugs/second curve: 0.1 at spawn, 4.25 at 60 seconds, 5 at 120 seconds, 6 at 240 seconds, then clamped. Downing, termination, removal and respawn clear that life's credits; revival resumes pressure at the original life age without spawn debt. Requests sample 32 seeded points uniformly by area in a 600–900-unit annulus around that player and choose the valid point farthest from the world centre. This keeps incoming groups toward the arena's outer regions, leaving interior openings for drop-ins. If none is valid, 64 angles at each of three radii provide the same bounded outer-region preference. Candidates must be in bounds, at least 500 units from every living player, and outside every living player's conservative 900-by-900 camera rectangle plus 64 units of padding. Bugs still target the nearest living player, so pressure is shared. Live bugs cap at 150, with one slot reserved for the scheduled hive (ordinary spawning caps at 149 while no hive lives); blocked whole requests are discarded without catchup debt. Stable room slots allocate requests starting after the last successful slot to avoid starvation at capacity.

Initial entry and respawn choose a nonoverlapping point with the greatest nearest-bug clearance from 64 seeded candidates and a 16-by-16 grid, all at least 450 units from every edge. With no bugs, central candidates are favoured. If no candidate has 300 units of clearance, every bug within 300 of the best point is pushed straight outward to 300; coincident centres use deterministic bug-id directions. Only positions change, with no kills or score. New lives receive two seconds of spawn protection, shown as a pale rotating shimmer. Emitting a shot ends that protection before contact resolution; blocked firing does not. Spawn protection is separate from the one-second immunity after damage, which firing never clears. Downed and out connections retain room slots and do not move, shoot or attract bugs. Only out players may respawn.

Protocol v1 adds `respawn` and `life_over`. Inputs carry increasing `seq`, `life` and boolean `touch`; stale-life inputs cannot affect a replacement life. Input and respawn attempts share the rolling 30/s budget and 1 KiB UTF-8 limit. Held inputs, including fire, expire after 250 ms. Full snapshots contain wave, player combat/life state (including status, downed deadline, cumulative revive progress and active contact), bugs, player shots, acid globs, floor pickups, active-effect deadlines and step hit/kill/bounce/glob-pop events. Bug events carry kind; glob-pop carries an independent fixed +5. Legacy JSON uses compact combat tuples with packet-local shot-owner indices, birth/tick ages and a shared entity-id base for large ids; decoding restores named objects before validation/consumption. Hive kill events contain up to twenty life-specific reward entries; the contribution ledger stays server-only. Hive objects retain maximum health and ring timing alongside compact ordinary bug tuples. JSON rounds numbers to three decimals; negotiated binary precision and recovery are described below; simulation state stays precise. Bounds are 20 players, 150 bugs, 24 globs, six floor pickups and 660 combined shots/impact events, within the independently tested 64 KiB server cap. Entity references and numeric bounds are validated on receipt. Snapshots encode once per room broadcast and reuse bytes across recipients; backed-up sockets skip snapshots and five-second stalls are evicted. Terminal results bypass cosmetic snapshot skipping and remain in dead-player snapshots.

`life_over` carries life identity, score, kills, precise simulation seconds, touch attribution and `pending`/`saved`/`failed` save status. Dead snapshots retain that frozen result. The client predicts active movement/facing only, resets prediction and controls across life/status changes and interpolates remote players, bugs and shots by identity. Combat uses one monotonic 100 ms delayed view for entity birth, position, removal and hit/kill/bounce effects. Historical membership retains even one-snapshot projectiles until their removal tick; the final interval advances at their last observed velocity. Hit and bounce sparks begin at the impact tick; kill bursts begin at the removal tick using the same animation-frame timestamp, and event ids prevent replay. Authoritative health, scores and life-over UI remain immediate. It draws cached robot glows/names, procedural beetles and shot streaks, a bounded kill-burst pool, heat/immunity flashes, level/score/multiplier/hits HUD and a life-over overlay. Old-life save responses cannot reopen the overlay after respawn. Downed robots lie dim with separate countdown/progress rings and a pulsing Help marker; off-screen teammates receive a CSS-pixel edge arrow. The local downed message and countdown retain the camera and keep terminal UI hidden.

The life-over screen immediately shows the authoritative score and save status with an always-available Play again button. Shared results components present server best, personal standing, points to the next rank (including inside the top 10) and team totals. `arena/results.ts` starts independent reads after a saved or failed acknowledgement and refreshes them every two seconds while visible, using the API's five-second timeout. Each read has its own failure state and retries; failed saves retain their warning while showing previously persisted standings. Overlay generations and per-field request sequences fence late responses across saves, respawns, disconnects and reused life numbers. Fresh matched pointer gestures under 10 px replay; scrolling, cancellation and held keys cannot. The work runs outside the frame loop and server tick. [Results validation](docs/arena-results-validation.md) records mixed-mode scoring, Chrome layouts, stats and live-hide evidence. Further bug kinds remain later slices.

`src/server/server.ts` owns server admission, sockets, timers and SQLite. `main.ts` supplies configuration/signals. Each spawn creates a server UUID run with `mode='arena'`; existing/HTTP runs default to `release1`. Start/finish and linked events commit atomically and retry idempotently. The stored legacy player-id casing is preserved. Finish writes server score, kills as `squashes`, floored simulation seconds, accumulated touch, team snapshot, empty upgrades and `held=0`. HTTP cannot finish arena runs; release 1 plausibility/review rules stay intact. An ordered between-tick lifecycle writer retains start metadata to recover failed starts, logs failures with run ids, updates save status and invalidates score caches after commits. Disconnect, moderation, stall eviction and shutdown finalize all unfinished lives, including downed ones, once. On SIGTERM/SIGINT, admission and simulation stop synchronously. Active lives finish with end_reason=restart (ordinary deaths use death; departures use disconnect; existing/release 1 rows keep null). Arena writes use zero SQLite lock timeout and a yielding shutdown drain retries lock contention for at most four seconds. Final saved/failed life results precede server_restart and close code 1012; sockets get up to 500 ms to close, with a 4.8-second process watchdog and nonzero exit for incomplete saves. Repeated signals share one drain. Reconnection starts a new life using the same identity and touch attribution, after 0.5, 1, 2, 4, then 5 seconds between attempts. The play view stays visible with truthful restart-save feedback. Vite resolves one build id from a nonempty build-time Railway commit, git HEAD, or UTC timestamp, embeds it in the browser and emits dist/build.json. Production reads that manifest once, ignoring runtime build overrides; without it the server uses development. A changed build triggers a guarded reload, with sessionStorage/history retaining identity and a one-use automatic arena resume marker. Failed reload attempts stay guarded across stale bundles; a welcome matching the requested page build retires those attempts so later rollbacks reload correctly. Pending restart results signal a connection-wide shutdown regardless of whether prediction has received their life number (including immediately after respawn). They freeze prediction and suspend the snapshot timeout until the final acknowledgement/close; ordinary stale death results remain life-filtered, and only committed results claim a saved score. If guard storage fails, reload is suppressed and logged.

`GET /api/arena/stats` reports buildId, populations, rolling nearest-rank tick p50/p95 and drop counters, with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Tick cost includes simulation, serialization and socket enqueueing; scheduler lateness is separate. Closed-room timing expires after a minute; delayed timers advance one step without catchup.

`bun run bots -- --url <owned-origin> --count 18 --seconds 60 --policy aimer` runs real WebSocket clients, validates snapshots, respawns by life identity and reports joins/rates/errors/tick/lateness. Policy defaults to aimer; mover is also available. Both use the same independently seeded policies as `bun run skill`: 20 seeds, eight bots, 180 seconds per policy, pooled life medians including horizon cutoffs. Aimer leads constant-velocity targets, prefers exposed shot angles, flanks guarded beetles and otherwise keeps distance, pauses at heat 12 until heat 3, and reads dasher wind-up corridors, retaining a perpendicular escape side through each dash (earliest tell, then id; choose world-edge clearance) and blending the dodge with crowd avoidance only while the corridor threatens; mover wanders/dodges and holds fire along its movement. The regression requires positive medians and aimer at least twice mover. The skill command also measures one newcomer's first life per seed against eight continuously respawning aimers: late joins at 480 seconds must reach 80% of the fresh-join median, and neutral-input medians at every 15-second arena age from 0 through 900 seconds plus solo controls at 0/480 must reach 10 seconds. Censored lives remain lower bounds, with paired windows extended from 600 to 1200/1800 seconds only if needed; inconclusive results fail. The ordinary check retains the existing skill regression, admission-observation tests and the previously failing idle cohorts at 90/150/180 seconds; the complete fairness matrix runs in the skill command. Press **F** for client FPS/update-and-draw cost. The HUD uses one line: score, multiplier, hits, personal level and the authoritative connected population in this arena, including dead connections. [Combat validation](docs/arena-combat-validation.md) records skill, production Chrome, simultaneous touch, full-room and 200-player evidence; [movement evidence](docs/arena-validation.md) retains the previous slice's history.

### Snapshot transport and load measurement

Browsers and bots request `snapshotEncoding: "delta-v1"` in `hello`; `welcome` confirms it. Legacy clients receive full JSON snapshots. Terminal/control messages remain JSON. The codec in `src/shared/arena/snapshot-codec.ts` reconstructs the existing complete `Snapshot`: all players, bugs, shots, globs, pickups, effects, events, acknowledgements and life results. The minimap and bot policies retain the complete arena.

Frames carry magic/version, keyframe/delta mode, arena id, current tick, delta base tick and wave. Collections carry births, removals and changes by stable identity, using references into the previous collection, bounded field masks, typed values and arithmetic varints. A per-frame string dictionary, seeded with player ids after the player collection, compresses repeated owners, kinds and phase labels with checked references. Reordering falls back to a keyframe. An extension cell retains additional kind-specific payloads and player effect maps without changing kind behaviours or registries. Pickups have their own bounded collection. Wire-only quantization is 1/8 world unit for positions, 1/16 unit/s for velocities, 1/1024 radian for player facing and 1/2048 for bug facing (the finer step preserves existing committed-motion validation), and three decimal places for other fractions. Integers (including ids, scores and ticks beyond 32 bits) remain exact; float fallback handles scaled values beyond the safe-integer range.

Each room keeps an immutable quantized baseline and reusable 64 KiB scratch writer. Each broadcast encodes one current keyframe and, when eligible, one delta, sharing immutable byte copies across recipients. Keyframes recur every 20 simulation ticks and on initial synchronization. Each connection records its last accepted snapshot tick. A skipped/rejected snapshot invalidates that connection's base, so its next accepted snapshot is a current keyframe. Accepted-but-queued sends are counted separately. Clients validate framing, collection limits, references and reconstructed state before committing a baseline. Missing bases preserve history, emit a diagnostic and wait for a keyframe; reconnects reset state. Decoded objects cannot mutate previous interpolation snapshots. Validated names are cached only within the current connection's player set.

Encoding/comparison work is bounded by existing caps (20 players, 150 bugs, 24 globs, six floor pickups and 660 combined shots/impact events), plus 20-recipient fan-out. Tick timing includes inputs, simulation, lifecycle enqueueing, encoding and socket enqueueing; scheduler lateness is separate. No compression, visibility filtering, reduced tick frequency or new gameplay cap is involved.

`GET /api/arena/stats` retains default rolling-minute timing and cumulative drops. Optional aligned `sinceMs`/`untilMs` (50 ms boundaries, half-open intervals, up to 180 seconds) return window traffic, connection/arena population exposure, per-arena/pooled timings, weighted snapshot-size histograms and drop deltas; `raw=1` includes timing samples. Server monotonic time and the earliest retained boundary are explicit. Expired, overwritten, pre-start or not-yet-completed windows have `complete: false`. Closed rooms and departed connections remain reportable until expiry. Percentiles pool observations rather than averaging room/window percentiles.

Recording uses reusable numeric rings and 50 ms traffic buckets; sorting and histogram construction occur only on reads. At 200 connections and ten arenas, fixed numeric reporting storage is approximately 151 MB plus object overhead; retention is 180 seconds. Departed records expire. The measured tick cost includes recording.

Bandwidth uses decimal KB (1 KB = 1,000 bytes), counting every application WebSocket payload accepted for enqueue, including queued snapshots and JSON controls. HTTP, WebSocket framing, TCP and TLS are excluded. The 40,000 bytes/s gate applies to each connection's mean over the complete window; one-second peaks are also reported. Client bytes are independently compared with server totals for the bot cohort, excluding other browsers. Clock uncertainty and boundary differences are printed. See [stream-scale proof](docs/arena-load.md) for measured per-recipient/per-tick sizes, fan-out, production results and factory Chrome evidence.

### Release target and extension seams

- **One process:** the existing Bun server also hosts the arenas. `Bun.serve` upgrades `GET /ws` to a WebSocket (Bun's built-in WebSocket, no new dependency). HTTP routes (Hono), SQLite and the static build stay as they are.
- **Shared simulation, server-run:** `src/shared/arena/` holds the arena simulation as pure TypeScript: world, players, bugs, shots, waves, pickups, scoring. It advances by a fixed step (50 ms, 20 Hz) from per-player inputs and a seeded random generator, never reading the clock, `Math.random` or the DOM, so `bun test` covers it by stepping. The server runs it; the client imports only its types, constants and pure helpers (for prediction and drawing).
- **Rooms:** `src/server/arena/` owns the room manager (arenas of at most 20 players; join the fullest arena with room, else open a new one; empty arenas close), the tick loop, the WebSocket protocol and the bridge to SQLite (a life's score is saved when the life ends, and for every live player on shutdown).
- **Protocol:** JSON control messages and negotiated binary snapshots defined once in `src/shared/arena/protocol.ts`, versioned, validated on receipt (unknown or oversized messages are dropped and counted). Client to server: `hello` (player id, name, team, touch flag, build id), `input` (sequence number, move vector, aim angle, fire held), `respawn`. Server to client: `welcome` (your id, arena id, build id, world size, constants), `snapshot` at 20 Hz (tick, players, bugs, shots, pickups, events since the last snapshot), `life_over` (life, score, kills, seconds, touch, save status, optional reason), `server_restart` (optional finalized restart result), `error`. Snapshots carry only what clients draw; one file per message type is fine.
- **Client:** `src/web/arena/` connects, sends inputs at the tick rate, interpolates other entities about 100 ms behind the latest snapshot and predicts its own robot's movement, reconciling with the server. The camera follows your robot over a world larger than the screen; a minimap shows teammates and the swarm. Rendering reuses the canvas look (procedural drawing, fixed pools, cached glows).
- **Controls:** keyboard + mouse (move, aim, hold to fire) and twin touch sticks (left half moves, right half aims and fires while held), in `src/web/arena/input.ts`.
- **Redeploy safety:** the server sends its packaged build id in `welcome` and stats; on `SIGTERM`/`SIGINT` it saves unfinished lives and sends finalized restart results before closing sockets with code 1012. Clients reconnect with backoff and resume automatically after a guarded changed-build reload. [Redeploy validation](docs/redeploy-validation.md) records production Chrome, persistence and deadline evidence.
- **Bots:** `scripts/bots.ts` (`bun run bots -- --url <base> --count <n> --seconds <s> [--policy mover|aimer]`) connects real WebSocket clients that play with a scripted policy, and reports an exact full window: joins, populations, directional message rates, accepted bytes, every player's mean/one-second peak, errors/drops, and per-arena/pooled tick p50/p95/max. Above 20 bots, cohorts of at most 20 sockets use worker threads while one collector reads contiguous intervals at most 30 seconds apart. All inputs start after admission and clock calibration, using monotonic deadlines without catch-up and the same seeded policies. The server remains one process. The same policies run inside the pure simulation for skill measurements in tests.
- **Seams so items don't collide:** one file per bug kind (`src/shared/arena/bugs/<kind>.ts`), per pickup (`src/shared/arena/pickups/<kind>.ts`) and per message type; a registry imports them. Bug extension steps are below; pickups likewise own their implementation, registration and tests.
- **Tests:** the simulation is tested by stepping; the room manager and protocol are tested in process; at least one integration test starts the real server on an ephemeral port (`port: 0`) and connects two WebSocket clients. Close every server and socket before a test ends.

### Bug seams

`src/shared/arena/bugs/registry.ts` is the authoritative kind catalog; `BugKind` derives from its keys. Every `BugDefinition` owns radius, maximum health, base points, initialization, deterministic behavior and kind-specific wire validation. `createBug` supplies required HP/facing and invokes initialization. `createBug` accepts the current simulation time so new kinds receive their full initial attack delay. Optional `phase`/`phaseUntil` are owned by the kind, with timers in simulation milliseconds. A kind can supply `advancePhase` to advance committed phase/timer/velocity state across missed snapshots without predicting health or target decisions; the same callback owns simulation transitions. Its `committedPhases` lists intervals with locked velocity, allowing rendering to advance their exact path before blending later tracking movement, even when the next snapshot removes the bug. Protocol checks common fields and checked registry membership before delegating phase/timer/velocity validation. Hit/kill/bounce events carry kind; glob-pop does not. All use at most one event per consumed player projectile, retaining the collection and 64 KiB bounds. Snapshot changes deploy client/server together through the existing build-id reload flow.

The dasher has radius 16, two HP and 25 base points. It approaches at 140 units/s, locks facing inside 240 units, stops for a 700 ms wind-up, dashes at 490 units/s for 450 ms without steering, then recovers at 35 units/s for 1,000 ms. It can take ordinary projectile damage in every phase. The 240-unit trigger is tuned from the suggested approximately 280: the dash travels 220.5 units and contact radii add 30. A committed attack completes even if its target dies. Nearest living targets break ties by id. A world edge stops the whole motion without sliding or changing locked facing; timers continue. Swept projectile and contact checks share reusable per-bug motion, split into travel then a stationary wall segment. Contact uses the player's actual step displacement; kills resolve first, surviving hits remain eligible for contact. Snapshots publish phase and velocity for the following interval after resolving the completed motion.

The spitter has radius 18, two HP and 30 base points. It corrects toward 400 units from the nearest active player, strafes at 35 units/s and caps combined speed at 140. Each 2,200 ms cycle moves for 1,700 ms, then stops and swells for 500 ms. At emission it aims at the nearest active player's current position, without lead. Targetless cycles are consumed. A server-only optional `attack` hook runs once after completed combat, damage and revives, before the next phase publication; movement and client phase advancement never emit entities. Killing a swelling spitter prevents its attack on that boundary.

`globs.ts` owns independent server projectiles: radius 9, muzzle offset 28, speed 320 units/s, lifetime 2,000 ms. They survive source death and disappear on first active-player contact, shot interception, expiry or world exit. Swept relative collisions use actual player displacement and clip to shot/glob lifetime and world exit. Stable player-shot order picks the earliest bug or eligible glob, breaking entity ties by id. Shot advancement splits at glob/player contact boundaries, with interception winning an exact tie before damage resolves. Contacts resolve by time, glob id and player id, consuming even when protection blocks damage. After each contact, current player status controls later contacts and pending shots: a downing cannot leave a stale interception cutoff. With no contacts the original single shot interval is retained. Bodies and globs share immunity/downing rules. A pop awards exactly 5 points without multiplying, increments accuracy streak for subsequent hits, and never increments bug kills. No persistence migration is needed. At most twelve spitter globs can coexist: both first-shot delay and repeat cadence exceed lifetime, so all living globs came from distinct spitters alive two seconds ago, including replacements. Twelve additional slots hold one hive ring. Hive globs carry `source: "hive"` and travel at 180 units/s; absent source retains spitter speed. The six-second ring delay exceeds the two-second lifetime, including across successive scheduled encounters.

Spitter swelling and glob birth/travel/removal use the delayed combat timeline. The swelling visual keeps collision radius unchanged. Cached green glows, a bright nine-unit glob core, a short velocity trail and green +5 interception bursts are procedural and bounded. The aimer checks closest approach over up to one second (clipped to remaining lifetime), holds a perpendicular escape side selected for wall clearance, blends it with crowd/wall/dasher avoidance, and resumes rescues once clear. Mover behavior is unchanged. [Spitter validation](docs/spitter-validation.md) records issue 78's evidence.

The hive lives in `bugs/hive.ts` and follows the registered bug recipe, with explicit scheduling/reward exceptions. Wave 5 starts at simulation time 120,000 ms; waves 10, 15 and subsequent fifth waves attempt one encounter before ordinary spawning. A surviving hive consumes later triggers without queuing replacements. Initial HP is frozen at `120 + 60 * active/downed players` (120 to 1,320); solo kills are allowed. Its 48-unit body stays within the world. Placement maximizes clearance from every connected player, including out players, by evaluating rectangle corners, edge/bisector intersections and interior circumcenters. Equal candidates use x then y. At least 600 units of clearance activates immediately; if the room covers every such point, a two-second stationary, untargetable, harmless arrival marker warns at the best available point instead. This exceptional search is bounded by twenty sites and runs only on scheduled arrivals.

The active hive moves at 80 units/s toward the centroid around the densest active player within 300 units, breaking cluster ties by distance then player id. Contact uses ordinary swept damage, protection and revival rules. Its server-only encounter record owns the four-second brood deadline and contribution ledger. The bug publishes the authoritative six-second `ringAt` deadline through the existing binary extension cell and JSON; rendering never reschedules it. Each brood attempts three ordinary crawlers just outside the shell within the shared 150-bug cap, consuming blocked requests without debt. Each ring emits twelve globs evenly spaced by pi/6, from its center, at 180 units/s. Attacks run after combat/revival; a lethal shot suppresses attacks due on that boundary. Brood and globs survive source death. Respawn displacement preserves encounter health, timers and credit.

Each authoritative hive impact credits actual HP removed to the shooter's current life. Ended/departed lives lose contributions immediately, revival retains them, and replacement/reconnected lives cannot inherit them. At death, eligible active/downed contributors each receive 50 points plus their damage-proportional share of a 300-point pool. Largest remainders (player-id ties) make the integer shares sum to exactly 300. Totals are `300 + 50N` (solo 350; four contributors 500), bypassing the accuracy multiplier. The lethal shooter still receives one kill, with zero ordinary kind-value payout. One kill event transports the awards/positions and drives delayed large floating scores and a two-second "Hive down!" banner. Awards are historical identities: validate UUIDs, positive safe life numbers, unique recipients, positions and conserved totals independently of current membership/life. A subsequent departure or respawn cannot invalidate or rewrite an earned award on an admission rebroadcast. The ledger is never serialized; existing life persistence/team attribution needs no migration.

Hive procedural shell/glow resources are cached; the pulse never changes collision size. During the final second before each ring, a distinct contracting amber circle, twelve world-aligned lane marks and "RING INCOMING" label use the delayed combat clock and authoritative deadline. Arrival has only its harmless arrival marker; the ring tell disappears at emission. Every screen receives the authoritative top health bar, delayed off-screen direction arrow and minimap diamond, including during arrival. The minimap and edge-arrow bounds move below the bar. A separate fixed twenty-entry award effect pool and the existing event identity prevent replay; connection reset clears both awards and banner. The compact celebration banner chooses a screen position clear of the award trajectories, including phone landscape. [Hive validation](docs/hive-validation.md) records issue 84's production Chrome, skill/fairness and load evidence.

`src/web/arena/bugs/registry.ts` is independently checked for every `BugKind`, so the server never imports canvas code. Each draw factory creates cached resources once; the main loop only culls by its registered extent and dispatches. The dasher's magenta angular shell compresses during wind-up, its cached hot glow brightens, and a faint locked-direction line shows the dash path, clipped to world bounds. Culling includes this warning extent. Animation uses the same delayed combat clock as positions, HP, phase changes and sparks; interpolation preserves stationary wind-up and straight dash intervals across gaps into recovery or removal, including stopping the whole motion at the first world edge. Later tracking movement is blended from that committed endpoint. HUD values remain authoritative and immediate.

The beetle in `bugs/beetle.ts` has radius 22, five HP, speed 84 (0.6 crawler), base points 60 and a maximum turn rate of 90 degrees/s. It tracks the nearest active player with id ties, stops without a target, and moves forward along its facing. `move` receives elapsed milliseconds only on the opening step call (50 ms); the closing velocity refresh defaults to zero, so it cannot turn twice. Facing remains fixed throughout each swept collision interval, starting from `createBug`'s deterministic facing zero.

`BugDefinition.blocksShot` is an optional allocation-free predicate for kind-owned defence. Combat supplies the normalized vector from the bug centre toward the projectile at earliest swept contact, using both bodies at that same instant and the inverse projectile direction for coincident centres. The beetle guards the inclusive +/-60 degree sector around its facing; side and rear contacts damage normally. Bounces spark at the shell surface, clamped to world bounds, and consume the projectile rather than reflecting it. Five uninterrupted damaging hits by a fresh shooter award 90 points; the multiplier caps that award at 180. The lethal shooter owns the kill when players share damage.

The beetle draw factory caches its glow and draws a bright gold front plate aligned to the guarded arc, animated legs and one additional fixed crack per lost HP. Cracks do not weaken armour. Pale 180 ms bounce sparks use the existing bounded effect pool and event-id deduplication, with no score label. HP, facing, cracks, projectile removal and feedback stay on the common delayed combat timeline; HUD streak and score remain immediate.

Aimer chooses the nearest interceptable exposed target (id ties), approximating centre-shot contact with observed facing and an extra angular margin. Closest danger still governs rescue safety and crowd avoidance. With only guarded targets it withholds fire and approaches an 85-unit orbit, retaining the tangent with more world-edge clearance. Velocity compensation and a strong tangent outrun the shell turn rate; selected beetle repulsion begins at 65 units while other threats retain their usual avoidance. Dasher corridor escape and safe rescues remain in place; mover is unchanged.

To add a kind:

1. Put tuning in `constants.ts` and its `BugDefinition` in `src/shared/arena/bugs/<kind>.ts`, with deterministic initialization/movement and phase-aware validation. Movement-only kinds need no attack hook; kinds that emit authoritative entities provide `attack`, which runs only at the server boundary before phase advancement. Register it in the simulation registry.
2. Add its procedural draw factory and culling extent in `src/web/arena/bugs/<kind>.ts`; register it in the draw registry. Cache canvas resources in the factory.
3. Add its wave-dependent weight in `bugs/spawn-mix.ts`. Scheduled bosses such as the hive have no mix weight and use the explicit wave-boundary hook instead. Ordinary placement/personal pressure remain shared; all kinds and brood share the live cap.
4. Test behavior, damage/value, wire states and drawing. Update the maximum-payload fixture and skill/performance evidence where the new state or work changes those bounds.

Kinds using the established movement, phase and shot-blocking hooks need only their own definition, draw factory, registry and spawn-mix entries plus tests. This beetle item establishes the missing armour hook and bounce event across combat, protocol and rendering; a future behaviour outside those hooks may require another explicit seam. Do not add a generic phase interpreter until actual behaviors require one. [Dasher validation](docs/dasher-validation.md) records issue 71's evidence. [Beetle validation](docs/beetle-validation.md) records issue 83's checks, skill results and production evidence.

A new attack entity also needs its collision, transport, delayed rendering and policy paths, as the spitter/glob implementation demonstrates.


### Pickup seams

`src/shared/arena/pickups/registry.ts` is the authoritative catalog; `PickupKind` derives from its keys. Each kind owns its effect and timing: spread fires three shots in a 20-degree fan for eight seconds; rapid multiplies firing rate by 1.8 (10.8 volleys/s) and halves heat per projectile for eight seconds; shield blocks one otherwise-damaging hit or expires after twenty seconds. Weapon effects combine and shield remains independent. Each spread pellet adds ordinary heat, halved while rapid is active; the final volley clamps heat to capacity before lockout. Collection replaces that kind's deadline, without stacking charges, pellet counts or remaining time. Cached angle arrays and scalar modifiers feed the shared weapon loop without per-shot modifier allocation.

Lethal ordinary-bug projectile hits roll once at the bug's swept impact position (crawler 2%, dasher 6%, spitter 6%, beetle 15%). Hive kills use their shared rewards without rolling this stream. The pickup stream starts at `nextRandom((seed ^ 0x9e3779b9) >>> 0)` and never consumes spawn randomness. A roll is consumed even at the six-pickup floor cap; successful rolls at capacity are discarded. With multiple registered kinds, a second roll selects uniformly only when a drop can be created. Floor state is `{ id, kind, x, y, expiresAt }`, using the shared entity id counter. New drops are born at the completed step boundary and expire 12 seconds later.

Each fixed step cleans expired floor/effect state, moves active players, then collects preexisting pickups before firing and damage. Swept circle entry uses actual player displacement and combined radius 30. The earliest touch wins, with lexicographic player-id ties independent of map insertion order; pickups resolve in id order, regardless of team. Combat drops cannot be collected until the next step. Boundary cleanup runs again before the completed snapshot: equality with a deadline means expired. Downing, termination and respawn clear effects; revival cannot restore them. `damagePlayer` is the shared enemy-damage entry point, for bug bodies and acid globs: status, spawn protection and hit immunity precede effect hooks. A blocked eligible hit grants the same one-second immunity as damage and stops further contacts that step. Firing retains shield.

Protocol v1 full snapshots require `pickups` and each player's compact `effects` map (registered kind to absolute expiry in simulation milliseconds). Validation bounds kinds, ids, world positions, active status and remaining durations. No collection command or effect event is sent. Combined rapid/spread firing bounds live shots and step impacts together at 660: an impact replaces a projectile, so it cannot count twice. The legacy JSON wire uses compact positional combat records (bugs; shots with an owner index into the same packet and birth age; events with tick age), plus an optional shared base to preserve large safe-integer entity ids compactly. Binary keyframes/deltas carry the same bounded pickup collection and effect maps. JSON position and velocity retain three-decimal precision, and life identity remains explicit. `parseServer` restores and validates named objects for all consumers; object-form fixtures pass the same validation. The payload fixture covers the full combined budget, all three effects on every active player, six mixed floor pickups, every downed/out split and terminal results under the unchanged 64 KiB cap. Client/server deploy together through build-id reloads.

The separate typed canvas registry caches resources at renderer construction. `pickups/assets.ts` pre-renders each kind's combined floor glow/glyph, robot overlay and static HUD icon into canvases once. Frame hooks blit those images; only the HUD's draining arc is drawn dynamically, with no per-frame gradients. Facing rotates the cached spread/rapid overlay; shield stays world-aligned. Floor glyphs use the monotonic delayed combat timeline for birth/removal and cull including glow extent; 250 ms bright/dim phases blink in the last three seconds. Shield uses a violet glyph and closed bubble, distinct from the pale rotating spawn-protection arcs. Spread uses a blue three-pronged glyph and fanned barrel; rapid uses a rose lightning glyph and hot barrel with bright vents. Robot hooks receive facing and paint above the robot body. Local and remote robot effects and the local HUD follow immediate authoritative state. The small glyph below the score line has a ring proportional to remaining duration; refresh resets it, consumption removes it. While a hive is visible, the icons move below its health bar, clear of the minimap. No collection is predicted.

Aimer bots seek the nearest safe pickup within 250 units (pickup-id ties), retaining independent aim and burst firing. Dasher/glob dodges, eligible rescues and committed beetle flanks take priority. Seeking requires travel time plus one step before expiry, no bug within 400 units, 350 units of route clearance, no intersection with a committed dash corridor including dodge clearance, and no predicted acid-glob collision along the proposed route. The detour clearances retain the policy's normal non-flanking 350-unit separation, including while a hive approaches in crowded late arenas. Safety is reevaluated each observation; same-kind refresh is allowed. Mover behavior is unchanged.

To add a pickup:

1. Add named tuning, a `PickupDefinition` in `src/shared/arena/pickups/<kind>.ts` and simulation tests for effect, duration and refresh; register the definition. Spread supplies cached fan offsets; rapid supplies fire-rate and heat multipliers.
2. Add its procedural floor/robot/HUD factory in `src/web/arena/pickups/<kind>.ts` and the typed draw registry; cache resources in the factory.
3. Extend payload fixtures and recalculate projectile/event bounds before any weapon modifier ships. Keep the snapshot cap at 64 KiB; test the shared projectile/impact budget and every effect combination.
4. Run checks and the full skill/fairness matrix, then production Chrome, full-arena renderer and 200-bot performance validation. [Pickup validation](docs/pickup-validation.md) records this slice's evidence.

## Stack
- Bun (runtime, package manager, test runner), TypeScript strict, Vite for the browser build, Canvas 2D for the game, Hono on Bun for a small API, SQLite through `bun:sqlite` for runs and scores.
- Why: the bootcamp's supported stack, with the same commands on Windows, macOS and Linux. A game loop needs no UI framework; the server runs combat, persistence and the shared boards.
- React is a thin shell for entry, first-life hints and results around the canvas. No React in the game loop.

## How it runs
- Install: `bun install`
- Develop: `bun run dev` (Vite dev server for the game, with the API server beside it)
- Start (production mode): `bun run build` then `bun run start`: one Bun process serves the API and built UI from `dist/` on `0.0.0.0` and `PORT` (default 3000). `bun run dev` keeps the API on `127.0.0.1`. `/board` (also `/board/`) is the stream display in both modes.
- Container: `Dockerfile` builds the game and runs `bun src/server/main.ts`; `DATA_DIR` must be a persistent volume.
- Health: `GET /health` answers `200 ok` once the app is ready.
- Sharing: `/favicon.svg` is a procedural robot; `/favicon.ico` embeds its 32 px PNG, `/apple-touch-icon.png` is 180 px, and `/og-image.png` is a 1200 x 630 arena composition. `src/server/share-assets.ts` generates these once at startup with the server-only `@resvg/resvg-js` rasterizer (no fonts or external resources), then serves cached bytes with `public, max-age=3600`. No generated art files are committed or loaded by gameplay. Vite forwards these exact routes to the API's `PORT`, including before the first build.
- Initial HTML includes the description, dark theme and Open Graph/Twitter cards. `PUBLIC_ORIGIN` takes precedence over `https://` plus Railway's `RAILWAY_PUBLIC_DOMAIN`, then the incoming request URL's origin in production. Configured origins are validated before opening SQLite; forwarded host/proto headers are ignored. Set `PUBLIC_ORIGIN=https://your-domain.example` for custom domains, or `http://your-lan-host:5173` for remote/LAN development. Only HTTP(S) origins are accepted, with an optional trailing slash. Local development needs no environment file: Vite uses its actual frontend URL/port. Built HTML keeps placeholders until the production server substitutes the origin, including `/index.html`, both board routes and SPA fallback; all cards describe the root game URL.
- Test everything: `bun run check` (`tsc --noEmit`, then `bun test`). CI runs exactly this.
- Multiplayer product check: `bun run playtest -- --issue <item-number>` (installed headless Chrome; `CHROME_PATH` override). Every arena gameplay, networking or UI change runs its relevant named scenarios and attaches the report to its PR. The factory product checker runs the full suite on every PR, separately from `check`.
- The bet's command: `bun run stats`. Moderation: `bun run review`, `bun run approve <run id>` and `bun run hide "<name>"`. Capacity: `bun run bots -- --url <owned-origin> --count 200 --seconds 120`. Pre-stream cleanup: `bun run reset -- --yes`.

## Data and compatibility

SQLite stores players, runs, linked events and hidden names. A run is one life; arena runs carry `mode='arena'`, authoritative combat scores, team snapshots and no upgrade choices. Existing rows and HTTP runs use `release1`. Schema changes remain additive. Public leaders, personal standings and team totals exclude held and hidden scores; personal bests and bet statistics retain historical held runs. Score caches expire within one second; board and results polling refresh every two seconds.

`POST /api/runs` and `POST /api/runs/:id/finish` remain for compatibility only. No shipped browser or workload tool calls them. Their 4 KiB JSON bodies, UUID ownership, fixed names/teams, elapsed-time bounds, upgrade payload validation, idempotency and review holds remain tested. Legacy scores must equal `10 * squashes + seconds`, seconds may not exceed 300 or whole server elapsed time, and squashes may not exceed `floor(4.5 * seconds)`. Compatibility starts charge rolling ten-minute IP/player budgets (300/40); `LOAD_TEST_START_LIMIT` can override the IP limit on a dedicated server. Arena admission uses the WebSocket protocol instead.

Read endpoints include `/api/leaderboard`, `/api/players/:id/best`, `/api/players/:id/standing`, `/api/teams` and `/api/arena/stats`. Standings return `{ rank, total, next }`, with null rank for unranked players and a strict-score gap to the next rank. Ranking ties use earliest eligible finish, then player id. Warm standing lookups use a shared cached player-id map. The board remains isolated at `/board` and `/board/`.

### Owner commands

Stats, hide, review, approve and reset use `DATA_DIR/app.db` (default `data/app.db`). Stats count accepted finishes only, never abandoned starts or legacy opens:

```sh
bun run stats
bun run stats -- --since 2026-11-28T09:00:00-06:00 --until 2026-11-28T10:15:00-06:00
bun run hide "Test Robot"
bun run review
bun run approve <run-id>
```

Filters may be used independently and require ISO timestamps with a timezone. Finish time is server time; since is inclusive and until is exclusive. The four stdout lines are `players`, `finished_runs`, `median_runs_per_player` (one decimal, over players with a finish), and `touch_share` (rounded whole percent). A missing database prints zeros without creating it. Bad flags fail to stderr with no partial report.

Review lists held finished runs oldest first (then run id), including run id, JSON-escaped name, score, submitted seconds, squashes and UTC ISO server finish time. Empty or missing databases print `No held runs`; review does not create a missing database. Approve accepts exactly one UUID, clears only that finished run's hold in an immediate transaction, and reports already eligible runs as a successful no-op. Bad arguments, missing databases and unknown or unfinished runs fail to stderr with exit 1. Both accept the optional `--` separator and close their database handles. Approval changes no score, event, timestamp or name moderation. Public boards, personal standings and team totals observe external approval within five seconds via existing cache expiry and polling, while reads succeed. Compatibility-only HTTP scores strictly above 3,000 require review; authoritative arena lives are saved without a hold.

Hide trims and lowercases the name, hides all players using it, and is idempotent. It accepts now-blocked names and never deletes history. A foreground game-over board observes it within five seconds through cache expiry and polling. Its persisted name key is unchanged by the separate moderation normalization.

Name moderation retains the trimmed 2–16-character ASCII letter/number/space/hyphen/underscore gate and display spelling. It then folds case, removes spaces, hyphens and underscores, maps 0/o, 1/i, 3/e, 4/a, 5/s, 7/t and 8/b, and collapses repeated letters in both names and vocabulary. Normalized blocked terms of four or more characters match substrings; shorter terms match complete names or complete names followed by digits (checked before leet folding), so `ass` is blocked without rejecting `Jason` or `classic`. Abusive compounds such as `badass` have explicit entries. Exact normalized complete-name exceptions allow ordinary collisions such as `Scunthorpe`, `Hancock`, `Draper`, `Sam Hancock` and `therapist`; extensions and numeric suffixes receive no exemption. Reserved names (`colemedin`, `cole medin`, `dynamous`, `archon`, `admin`, `moderator`) use normalized equality before exceptions and return exactly “That name is reserved”; `ArchonFan` and `AdminHelper` remain eligible. Profanity retains “That name isn't allowed. Pick another.” Vocabulary maintenance instructions and the matching tradeoff live beside the list. Updated vocabulary takes effect when the application is deployed, including read-time exclusion of legacy players from both boards, without changing history, personal bests or bet stats. Manual-hide cache expiry and polling remain as above. The entry screen maps both moderation errors to "Pick a different name." and format errors to "Use 2 to 16 letters or numbers." only after a join attempt; editing clears the message. No moderation runs in the frame loop.

`bun run reset` refuses until passed exactly `--yes` (optionally after `--`). The refusal names the resolved database path. A confirmed reset deletes **all** players, runs (including abandoned ones) and events (including legacy opens) in one immediate transaction. It preserves hidden-name moderation, tables and indexes, and prints deleted counts after commit. A missing database succeeds with zeros without creating files. Stop the server before a pre-stream reset and restart afterwards to clear read caches and in-memory rate-limit buckets; unset the load-test override before restarting. Reset does not selectively remove `load-` names.
