# Architecture

## Arena

The game is a server-authoritative multiplayer arena (see `direction.md` and `PRD.md`). Release 1 gameplay has been retired; only compatibility HTTP endpoints and historical data remain.

### Available now: aim, fire and authoritative lives

`/` opens the shared StartScreen and joins a 2400 by 2400 world, packed up to 20 players per room. `/arena` and `/arena/` temporarily redirect to `/`, retaining query parameters. First entry remains an editable draft until `welcome` stores the identity. Permanent names/teams, duplicate-UUID refusal, case-insensitive legacy UUID lookup and live name moderation remain enforced. The one-second moderation scan evicts hidden names with `error` ("That name isn't allowed.") and socket closure; future joins receive the same message.

Players move with WASD/arrows and aim with a world-space mouse cursor; left mouse or Space holds fire. Touch owns independent floating move and aim/fire sticks, with a 6 px dead zone and 60 px radius. Ownership stays with each pointer across screen crossings. Blur, capture loss, rotation, disconnect and death clear controls. A fresh tap, Enter, Space or movement key respawns; held inputs at death cannot restart a life. Shared DOM-free view geometry bounds the longest world-view dimension to 900 units, scaling world drawing and mouse coordinates while keeping HUD and sticks in CSS pixels. Bounded camera padding outside the world keeps the local name, barrel and heat feedback clear of the HUD and screen edges at every boundary; movement remains inside the world. The 450-unit spawn inset keeps newly joined robots centred in every supported view.

The pure seeded simulation advances one 50 ms step. `constants.ts` owns tuning: shots travel at 900 units/s, six per second, for 900 ms; barrel offset 25, shot radius 3. Fractional shot cadence and partial-step travel are preserved. Releasing fire keeps an unexpired cooldown and discards overdue fire, so rapid clicking cannot increase the rate or bank a burst. Swept relative-circle collisions pick the earliest bug, with bug-id ties and stable shot order. Players never block shots. An expiry or world exit resets the hit streak once. Each hit increases the multiplier by 0.1, capped at 3, before awarding the crawler's 10 points; earned points survive misses.

Each shot adds one heat. Capacity 18 locks firing for 1500 ms. Heat cools at six per second while released or locked, including fractional lockout steps, and never cools between shots in a held stream. Crawlers live in `bugs/crawler.ts`, imported through the registry: speed 140, radius 12, one hit point, nearest living target with player-id ties. Contact costs one of three hits with one second of immunity, leaves the bug alive and never scores. The third hit ends the life immediately; teammate revives are a later item.

Wave 1 begins on the room's first step and advances every 30 seconds. Waves are an event/unlock clock and do not add spawn volume; other bug kinds and bosses remain future items. Each living player's own life generates spawn credits by integrating a piecewise-linear bugs/second curve: 0.1 at spawn, 4 at 60 seconds, 5 at 120 seconds, 6 at 240 seconds, then clamped. Death, removal and respawn clear that life's credits. Requests sample 32 seeded points uniformly by area in a 600–900-unit annulus around that player and choose the valid point farthest from the world centre. This keeps incoming groups toward the arena's outer regions, leaving interior openings for drop-ins. If none is valid, 64 angles at each of three radii provide the same bounded outer-region preference. Candidates must be in bounds, at least 500 units from every living player, and outside every living player's conservative 900-by-900 camera rectangle plus 64 units of padding. Bugs still chase the nearest living player, so pressure is shared. Live bugs cap at 150; blocked whole requests are discarded without catchup debt. Stable room slots allocate requests starting after the last successful slot to avoid starvation at capacity.

Initial entry and respawn choose a nonoverlapping point with the greatest nearest-bug clearance from 64 seeded candidates and a 16-by-16 grid, all at least 450 units from every edge. With no bugs, central candidates are favoured. If no candidate has 300 units of clearance, every bug within 300 of the best point is pushed straight outward to 300; coincident centres use deterministic bug-id directions. Only positions change, with no kills or score. New lives receive two seconds of spawn protection, shown as a pale rotating shimmer. Emitting a shot ends that protection before contact resolution; blocked firing does not. Spawn protection is separate from the one-second immunity after damage, which firing never clears. Dead connections retain room slots and do not move, shoot or attract bugs.

Protocol v1 adds `respawn` and `life_over`. Inputs carry increasing `seq`, `life` and boolean `touch`; stale-life inputs cannot affect a replacement life. Input and respawn attempts share the rolling 30/s budget and 1 KiB UTF-8 limit. Held inputs, including fire, expire after 250 ms. Full snapshots contain wave, player combat/life state, bugs, shots and step kill events. The wire rounds numbers to three decimals; simulation state stays precise. Bounds are 20 players, 150 bugs, 140 shots and 140 events, within the independently tested 64 KiB server cap. Entity references and numeric bounds are validated on receipt. Snapshots serialize once per room tick, skip backed-up sockets and evict five-second stalls. Terminal results bypass cosmetic snapshot skipping and remain in dead-player snapshots.

`life_over` carries life identity, score, kills, precise simulation seconds, touch attribution and `pending`/`saved`/`failed` save status. Dead snapshots retain that frozen result. The client predicts living movement/facing only, resets prediction across life changes and interpolates remote players, bugs and shots by identity. Combat uses one monotonic 100 ms delayed view for entity birth, position, removal and kill effects. Historical membership retains even one-snapshot projectiles until their removal tick; the final interval advances at their last observed velocity. Kill bursts begin at that removal tick using the same animation-frame timestamp, and event ids prevent replay. Authoritative health, scores and life-over UI remain immediate. It draws cached robot glows/names, procedural beetles and shot streaks, a bounded kill-burst pool, heat/immunity flashes, wave/score/multiplier/hits HUD and a life-over overlay. Old-life save responses cannot reopen the overlay after respawn.

The life-over screen immediately shows the authoritative score and save status with an always-available Play again button. Shared results components present server best, personal standing, points to the next rank (including inside the top 10) and team totals. `arena/results.ts` starts independent reads after a saved or failed acknowledgement and refreshes them every two seconds while visible, using the API's five-second timeout. Each read has its own failure state and retries; failed saves retain their warning while showing previously persisted standings. Overlay generations and per-field request sequences fence late responses across saves, respawns, disconnects and reused life numbers. Fresh matched pointer gestures under 10 px replay; scrolling, cancellation and held keys cannot. The work runs outside the frame loop and server tick. [Results validation](docs/arena-results-validation.md) records mixed-mode scoring, Chrome layouts, stats and live-hide evidence. Other bug kinds, revives and pickups remain later slices.

`src/server/server.ts` owns server admission, sockets, timers and SQLite. `main.ts` supplies configuration/signals. Each spawn creates a server UUID run with `mode='arena'`; existing/HTTP runs default to `release1`. Start/finish and linked events commit atomically and retry idempotently. The stored legacy player-id casing is preserved. Finish writes server score, kills as `squashes`, floored simulation seconds, accumulated touch, team snapshot, empty upgrades and `held=0`. HTTP cannot finish arena runs; release 1 plausibility/review rules stay intact. An ordered between-tick lifecycle writer retains start metadata to recover failed starts, logs failures with run ids, updates save status and invalidates score caches after commits. Disconnect, moderation, stall eviction and shutdown finalize active lives once. On SIGTERM/SIGINT, admission and simulation stop synchronously. Active lives finish with end_reason=restart (ordinary deaths use death; departures use disconnect; existing/release 1 rows keep null). Arena writes use zero SQLite lock timeout and a yielding shutdown drain retries lock contention for at most four seconds. Final saved/failed life results precede server_restart and close code 1012; sockets get up to 500 ms to close, with a 4.8-second process watchdog and nonzero exit for incomplete saves. Repeated signals share one drain. Reconnection starts a new life using the same identity and touch attribution, after 0.5, 1, 2, 4, then 5 seconds between attempts. The play view stays visible with truthful restart-save feedback. Vite resolves one build id from a nonempty build-time Railway commit, git HEAD, or UTC timestamp, embeds it in the browser and emits dist/build.json. Production reads that manifest once, ignoring runtime build overrides; without it the server uses development. A changed build triggers a guarded reload, with sessionStorage/history retaining identity and a one-use automatic arena resume marker. Failed reload attempts stay guarded across stale bundles; a welcome matching the requested page build retires those attempts so later rollbacks reload correctly. Pending restart results signal a connection-wide shutdown regardless of whether prediction has received their life number (including immediately after respawn). They freeze prediction and suspend the snapshot timeout until the final acknowledgement/close; ordinary stale death results remain life-filtered, and only committed results claim a saved score. If guard storage fails, reload is suppressed and logged.

`GET /api/arena/stats` reports buildId, populations, rolling nearest-rank tick p50/p95 and drop counters, with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Tick cost includes simulation, serialization and socket enqueueing; scheduler lateness is separate. Closed-room timing expires after a minute; delayed timers advance one step without catchup.

`bun run bots -- --url <owned-origin> --count 18 --seconds 60 --policy aimer` runs real WebSocket clients, validates snapshots, respawns by life identity and reports joins/rates/errors/tick/lateness. Policy defaults to aimer; mover is also available. Both use the same independently seeded policies as `bun run skill`: 20 seeds, eight bots, 180 seconds per policy, pooled life medians including horizon cutoffs. Aimer leads constant-velocity targets, keeps distance and pauses at heat 12 until heat 3; mover wanders/dodges and holds fire along its movement. The regression requires positive medians and aimer at least twice mover. The skill command also measures one newcomer's first life per seed against eight continuously respawning aimers: late joins at 480 seconds must reach 80% of the fresh-join median, and neutral-input medians at every 15-second arena age from 0 through 900 seconds plus solo controls at 0/480 must reach 10 seconds. Censored lives remain lower bounds, with paired windows extended from 600 to 1200/1800 seconds only if needed; inconclusive results fail. The ordinary check retains the existing skill regression, admission-observation tests and the previously failing idle cohorts at 90/150/180 seconds; the complete fairness matrix runs in the skill command. Press **F** for client FPS/update-and-draw cost. The HUD uses one line: score, multiplier, hits, wave and the authoritative connected population in this arena, including dead connections. [Combat validation](docs/arena-combat-validation.md) records skill, production Chrome, simultaneous touch, full-room and 200-player evidence; [movement evidence](docs/arena-validation.md) retains the previous slice's history.

### Release target and extension seams

- **One process:** the existing Bun server also hosts the arenas. `Bun.serve` upgrades `GET /ws` to a WebSocket (Bun's built-in WebSocket, no new dependency). HTTP routes (Hono), SQLite and the static build stay as they are.
- **Shared simulation, server-run:** `src/shared/arena/` holds the arena simulation as pure TypeScript: world, players, bugs, shots, waves, pickups, scoring. It advances by a fixed step (50 ms, 20 Hz) from per-player inputs and a seeded random generator, never reading the clock, `Math.random` or the DOM, so `bun test` covers it by stepping. The server runs it; the client imports only its types, constants and pure helpers (for prediction and drawing).
- **Rooms:** `src/server/arena/` owns the room manager (arenas of at most 20 players; join the fullest arena with room, else open a new one; empty arenas close), the tick loop, the WebSocket protocol and the bridge to SQLite (a life's score is saved when the life ends, and for every live player on shutdown).
- **Protocol:** small JSON messages defined once in `src/shared/arena/protocol.ts`, versioned, validated on receipt (unknown or oversized messages are dropped and counted). Client to server: `hello` (player id, name, team, touch flag, build id), `input` (sequence number, move vector, aim angle, fire held), `respawn`. Server to client: `welcome` (your id, arena id, build id, world size, constants), `snapshot` at 20 Hz (tick, players, bugs, shots, pickups, events since the last snapshot), `life_over` (life, score, kills, seconds, touch, save status, optional reason), `server_restart` (optional finalized restart result), `error`. Snapshots carry only what clients draw; one file per message type is fine.
- **Client:** `src/web/arena/` connects, sends inputs at the tick rate, interpolates other entities about 100 ms behind the latest snapshot and predicts its own robot's movement, reconciling with the server. The camera follows your robot over a world larger than the screen; a minimap shows teammates and the swarm. Rendering reuses the canvas look (procedural drawing, fixed pools, cached glows).
- **Controls:** keyboard + mouse (move, aim, hold to fire) and twin touch sticks (left half moves, right half aims and fires while held), in `src/web/arena/input.ts`.
- **Redeploy safety:** the server sends its packaged build id in `welcome` and stats; on `SIGTERM`/`SIGINT` it saves active lives and sends finalized restart results before closing sockets with code 1012. Clients reconnect with backoff and resume automatically after a guarded changed-build reload. [Redeploy validation](docs/redeploy-validation.md) records production Chrome, persistence and deadline evidence.
- **Bots:** `scripts/bots.ts` (`bun run bots -- --url <base> --count <n> --seconds <s> [--policy mover|aimer]`) connects real WebSocket clients that play with a scripted policy, and reports joins, message rates, errors and the server's tick p50/p95 (the server exposes tick timing on `GET /api/arena/stats`). The same policies run inside the pure simulation for skill measurements in tests.
- **Seams so items don't collide:** one file per bug kind (`src/shared/arena/bugs/<kind>.ts`), per pickup (`src/shared/arena/pickups/<kind>.ts`) and per message type; a registry imports them. Adding a bug or pickup touches its own file, the registry and its test, nothing else.
- **Tests:** the simulation is tested by stepping; the room manager and protocol are tested in process; at least one integration test starts the real server on an ephemeral port (`port: 0`) and connects two WebSocket clients. Close every server and socket before a test ends.

### Entry and screen overlays

`src/web/entry/StartScreen.tsx` shows the title, name and two team buttons; returning players see their name/team and one Play button. Legacy teamless identities choose a team once. Arena owns draft/error state, touch attribution and welcome-only identity persistence. No socket opens before activation except a one-use deploy resume. Rejected fresh names remain editable; accepted identities stay fixed. `src/web/identity.ts` retains the `bug-swarm-player` key and UUID spelling. `src/web/api.ts` reads scores only. `src/web/drawing/` owns the decorative scene/share styles and drawing primitives, independently of simulation.

`arena/minimap.ts` owns projection and all minimap markers so new markers can be added in one place. It preserves world aspect, outlines the world, shows faint bug dots and both living teams, replaces the local interpolated entry with predicted position and draws its bright ring last. It visits at most 20 players and 150 bugs. HUD formatting/measurement is cached until values or viewport change; only overflowing scores use compact notation. Safe-area padding keeps canvas overlays within the usable screen; touch sticks draw above them.

`arena/hint.ts` consumes `bug-swarm-arena-hint-seen=1` on the first living authoritative snapshot. A noninteractive two-line desktop/touch hint fades after six seconds or the first confirmed shot (`spawnProtectedUntil === 0`); natural protection expiry does not dismiss it. Touch can update the visible copy. Death, disconnect and unmount dismiss it; reconnect and respawn never rearm it. Storage failures log once and retain page-lifetime suppression. React updates only on hint transitions. Validation: [root cutover](docs/cutover-validation.md).

## Stack
- Bun (runtime, package manager, test runner), TypeScript strict, Vite for the browser build, Canvas 2D for the game, Hono on Bun for a small API, SQLite through `bun:sqlite` for runs and scores.
- Why: the bootcamp's supported stack, with the same commands on Windows, macOS and Linux. A game loop needs no UI framework; the server runs combat, persistence and the shared boards.
- React is a thin shell for entry, first-life hints and results around the canvas. No React in the game loop.

## How it runs
- Install: `bun install`
- Develop: `bun run dev` (Vite dev server for the game, with the API server beside it)
- Start (production mode): `bun run build` then `bun run start`: one Bun process serves the API and built UI from `dist/` on `0.0.0.0` and `PORT` (default 3000). `bun run dev` keeps the API on `127.0.0.1`. `/board` (also `/board/`) is the stream display in both modes.
- Container: `Dockerfile` builds the game and runs `bun src/server/main.ts`; `DATA_DIR` must be a persistent volume.
- Health: `GET /health` answers `200 ok` once the app is ready.
- Sharing: `/favicon.svg` is a procedural robot; `/favicon.ico` embeds its 32 px PNG, `/apple-touch-icon.png` is 180 px, and `/og-image.png` is a 1200 x 630 arena composition. `src/server/share-assets.ts` generates these once at startup with the server-only `@resvg/resvg-js` rasterizer (no fonts or external resources), then serves cached bytes with `public, max-age=3600`. No generated art files are committed or loaded by gameplay. Vite forwards these exact routes to the API's `PORT`, including before the first build.
- Initial HTML includes the description, dark theme and Open Graph/Twitter cards. `PUBLIC_ORIGIN` takes precedence over `https://` plus Railway's `RAILWAY_PUBLIC_DOMAIN`, then the incoming request URL's origin in production. Configured origins are validated before opening SQLite; forwarded host/proto headers are ignored. Set `PUBLIC_ORIGIN=https://your-domain.example` for custom domains, or `http://your-lan-host:5173` for remote/LAN development. Only HTTP(S) origins are accepted, with an optional trailing slash. Local development needs no environment file: Vite uses its actual frontend URL/port. Built HTML keeps placeholders until the production server substitutes the origin, including `/index.html`, both board routes and SPA fallback; all cards describe the root game URL.
- Test everything: `bun run check` (`tsc --noEmit`, then `bun test`). CI runs exactly this.
- The bet's command: `bun run stats`. Moderation: `bun run review`, `bun run approve <run id>` and `bun run hide "<name>"`. Capacity: `bun run bots -- --url <owned-origin> --count 200 --seconds 120`. Pre-stream cleanup: `bun run reset -- --yes`.

### Container

Build and run with a persistent data directory:

```sh
docker build -t bug-swarm .
docker volume create bug-swarm-data
docker run --rm --name bug-swarm-smoke -e PORT=8080 -e DATA_DIR=/data -p 8080:8080 -v bug-swarm-data:/data bug-swarm
```

Open `http://localhost:8080/`; `http://localhost:8080/health` answers `200 ok`.
The image builds the web bundle and runs `bun src/server/main.ts` directly so Bun receives platform signals. Railway supplies RAILWAY_GIT_COMMIT_SHA as a build argument.
Without `DATA_DIR`, the default relative `data/` directory resolves to `/app/data` in the image.
Persistent deployments should mount their chosen `DATA_DIR`; the example stores `/data/app.db` in the named volume and reuses it across restarts.


## Data and compatibility

SQLite stores players, runs, linked events and hidden names. A run is one life; arena runs carry `mode='arena'`, authoritative combat scores, team snapshots and no upgrade choices. Existing rows and HTTP runs use `release1`. Schema changes remain additive. Public leaders, personal standings and team totals exclude held and hidden scores; personal bests and bet statistics retain historical held runs. Score caches expire within one second; board and results polling refresh every two seconds.

`POST /api/runs` and `POST /api/runs/:id/finish` remain for compatibility only. No shipped browser or workload tool calls them. Their 4 KiB JSON bodies, UUID ownership, fixed names/teams, elapsed-time bounds, upgrade payload validation, idempotency and review holds remain tested. Legacy scores must equal `10 * squashes + seconds`, seconds may not exceed 300 or whole server elapsed time, and squashes may not exceed `floor(4.5 * seconds)`. Compatibility starts charge rolling ten-minute IP/player budgets (300/40); `LOAD_TEST_START_LIMIT` can override the IP limit on a dedicated server. Arena admission uses the WebSocket protocol instead.

Read endpoints include `/api/leaderboard`, `/api/players/:id/best`, `/api/players/:id/standing`, `/api/teams` and `/api/arena/stats`. Standings return `{ rank, total, next }`, with null rank for unranked players and a strict-score gap to the next rank. Ranking ties use earliest eligible finish, then player id. Warm standing lookups use a shared cached player-id map. The board remains isolated at `/board` and `/board/`.

### Owner commands

Stats, hide, review, approve and reset use `DATA_DIR/app.db` (default `data/app.db`). Stats count accepted finishes only, never abandoned starts or legacy opens:

```sh
bun run stats
bun run stats -- --since 2026-11-28T09:00:00-06:00 --until 2026-11-28T10:15:00-06:00
bun run hide "Test Robot"
bun run review
bun run approve <run-id>
```

Filters may be used independently and require ISO timestamps with a timezone. Finish time is server time; since is inclusive and until is exclusive. The four stdout lines are `players`, `finished_runs`, `median_runs_per_player` (one decimal, over players with a finish), and `touch_share` (rounded whole percent). A missing database prints zeros without creating it. Bad flags fail to stderr with no partial report.

Review lists held finished runs oldest first (then run id), including run id, JSON-escaped name, score, submitted seconds, squashes and UTC ISO server finish time. Empty or missing databases print `No held runs`; review does not create a missing database. Approve accepts exactly one UUID, clears only that finished run's hold in an immediate transaction, and reports already eligible runs as a successful no-op. Bad arguments, missing databases and unknown or unfinished runs fail to stderr with exit 1. Both accept the optional `--` separator and close their database handles. Approval changes no score, event, timestamp or name moderation. Public boards, personal standings and team totals observe external approval within five seconds via existing cache expiry and polling, while reads succeed. Compatibility-only HTTP scores strictly above 3,000 require review; authoritative arena lives are saved without a hold.

Hide trims and lowercases the name, hides all players using it, and is idempotent. It accepts now-blocked names and never deletes history. A foreground game-over board observes it within five seconds through cache expiry and polling. Its persisted name key is unchanged by the separate moderation normalization.

Name moderation retains the trimmed 2–16-character ASCII letter/number/space/hyphen/underscore gate and display spelling. It then folds case, removes spaces, hyphens and underscores, maps 0/o, 1/i, 3/e, 4/a, 5/s, 7/t and 8/b, and collapses repeated letters in both names and vocabulary. Normalized blocked terms of four or more characters match substrings; shorter terms match complete names or complete names followed by digits (checked before leet folding), so `ass` is blocked without rejecting `Jason` or `classic`. Abusive compounds such as `badass` have explicit entries. Exact normalized complete-name exceptions allow ordinary collisions such as `Scunthorpe`, `Hancock`, `Draper`, `Sam Hancock` and `therapist`; extensions and numeric suffixes receive no exemption. Reserved names (`colemedin`, `cole medin`, `dynamous`, `archon`, `admin`, `moderator`) use normalized equality before exceptions and return exactly “That name is reserved”; `ArchonFan` and `AdminHelper` remain eligible. Profanity retains “That name isn't allowed. Pick another.” Vocabulary maintenance instructions and the matching tradeoff live beside the list. Updated vocabulary takes effect when the application is deployed, including read-time exclusion of legacy players from both boards, without changing history, personal bests or bet stats. Manual-hide cache expiry and polling remain as above. The entry screen maps both moderation errors to "Pick a different name." and format errors to "Use 2 to 16 letters or numbers." only after a join attempt; editing clears the message. No moderation runs in the frame loop.

`bun run reset` refuses until passed exactly `--yes` (optionally after `--`). The refusal names the resolved database path. A confirmed reset deletes **all** players, runs (including abandoned ones) and events (including legacy opens) in one immediate transaction. It preserves hidden-name moderation, tables and indexes, and prints deleted counts after commit. A missing database succeeds with zeros without creating files. Stop the server before a pre-stream reset and restart afterwards to clear read caches and in-memory rate-limit buckets; unset the load-test override before restarting. Reset does not selectively remove `load-` names.

## Hosting
- Railway, from the `Dockerfile`, with a volume mounted at `DATA_DIR` and GitHub auto-deploy on `main`, so a merged change reaches players within minutes. One instance (SQLite needs one long-running process with a disk). The owner sets up the Railway project; the app only has to honor `PORT` and `DATA_DIR`.
- For a single Railway ingress that appends the actual caller's address to the right of `X-Forwarded-For`, use `TRUST_PROXY_HOPS=1`. Verify that ingress assumption using the procedure below before enabling it; local tests prove application selection, not Railway's header ordering.

### Pre-stream proxy verification

Use an isolated Railway deployment with scratch data and the same ingress configuration as the stream deployment. Temporarily log the TCP peer, complete `X-Forwarded-For` chain and selected client address at the request boundary. Send requests from a known network with (1) no supplied header, (2) `X-Forwarded-For: 192.0.2.10`, and (3) `X-Forwarded-For: 192.0.2.10, 192.0.2.11`. Confirm that the rightmost entry is always the actual caller address and that selection with one trusted hop is unchanged by those forged prefixes. Repeat from another network and confirm the selected address changes to that caller. Remove the temporary diagnostics afterward. If the ingress replaces or orders the header differently, record the observed mismatch for correction and do not enable the setting or claim deployment acceptance. Railway ingress has not been verified by the local application tests.
